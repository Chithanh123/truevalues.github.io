### CR-13 — Disability, independence, and effective access

Does independence mean acting without help, directing needed help, or having effective opportunities; and when do equal rules fail to provide access?

**Competing arguments and disagreements within traditions:**

- **Disability rights and relational autonomy:** Directing assistance can preserve agency; unequal expenditure may be needed for usable opportunities. Internal disagreement: Disability-led approaches differ over individual budgets, collective provision, professional expertise, and the adequacy of rights language. No single item represents the movement.
- **Capability approaches and public-service equality:** Evaluate what people can actually do, not only identical inputs or formal rules. Internal disagreement: Capability accounts differ over priority setting, personal responsibility, the relevant capabilities, and limits on public resources.
- **Procedural equality and administrative predictability:** Published standards can constrain arbitrary discretion and make obligations feasible to administer. Internal disagreement: A rule-based approach may still require exceptions and accommodation; it need not deny the reality of unmet needs.

**Reading connections:** [EX-R01](source-evidence.md#ex-r01), [EX-R06](source-evidence.md#ex-r06), [EX-R12](source-evidence.md#ex-r12)

#### CR-13A · Conceptual / ontological

**EN:** Being able to direct help from other people is a form of independence, even when a person cannot carry out everyday tasks alone.

**VI — author draft:** Có thể tự quyết định cách người khác hỗ trợ mình là một dạng độc lập, ngay cả khi một người không thể tự làm các công việc hằng ngày.

**Added distinction:** Independence as authority within supportive relationships, rather than physical self-sufficiency. Adds the meaning of independence to CR-04's allocation of care.

**Rival reading:** Agreement may concern practical convenience rather than relational autonomy; disagreement may reserve the word independence for doing tasks alone while supporting equal decision rights.

**Comprehension probe:** Could someone need daily assistance and still decide how to live? Does your answer change their authority or only the word you use?

**Reading connections:** [EX-R01](source-evidence.md#ex-r01), [EX-R12](source-evidence.md#ex-r12)

#### CR-13B · Normative / institutional

**EN:** Public disability services should pay for a person's chosen support arrangement even when an equally safe arrangement would cost less.

**VI — author draft:** Dịch vụ hỗ trợ người khuyết tật bằng ngân sách công nên chi trả cho cách hỗ trợ mà người đó lựa chọn, ngay cả khi có cách khác cũng an toàn như vậy nhưng ít tốn kém hơn.

**Added distinction:** Weight placed on the user's choice when safety is held equal and resources conflict. No particular price difference or unlimited entitlement is implied.

**Rival reading:** Agreement may assume the extra cost is small; disagreement may reflect opportunity costs to other users rather than distrust of disabled people's choices.

**Comprehension probe:** How much extra cost did you imagine? What if the cheaper arrangement provided fewer opportunities despite being equally safe?

**Reading connections:** [EX-R01](source-evidence.md#ex-r01), [EX-R12](source-evidence.md#ex-r12)

#### CR-13C · Normative / distributive

**EN:** When equal spending can either improve a public service for many people or enable a few people currently excluded to use it, enabling access for the excluded few should take priority.

**VI — author draft:** Khi cùng một khoản chi có thể cải thiện dịch vụ công cho nhiều người hoặc giúp một số ít người đang bị loại trừ sử dụng được dịch vụ, việc giúp số ít người đó tiếp cận dịch vụ nên được ưu tiên.

**Added distinction:** Priority for crossing a participation threshold versus aggregate improvement, distinct from equal quantities of support.

**Rival reading:** Agreement may depend on an imagined legal obligation; disagreement may depend on an imagined life-saving improvement for the larger group. The stem does not establish those facts.

**Comprehension probe:** What kind of service and improvement did you imagine? Would your answer differ if everyone already had access to the service?

**Reading connections:** [EX-R01](source-evidence.md#ex-r01), [EX-R12](source-evidence.md#ex-r12)

#### CR-13D · Normative / institutional

**EN:** A public service has met its access obligations if it follows published accessibility standards, even when some disabled people still cannot use it.

**VI — author draft:** Một dịch vụ công đã hoàn thành nghĩa vụ bảo đảm khả năng tiếp cận nếu tuân thủ các tiêu chuẩn tiếp cận được công bố, ngay cả khi một số người khuyết tật vẫn không thể sử dụng dịch vụ đó.

**Added distinction:** Compliance with a specified standard versus effective access for particular users. WCAG supplies an example that formal conformance can leave needs unmet, not an answer to this normative claim.

**Rival reading:** Disagreement can reflect doubts about the standard's adequacy; agreement can concern legal certainty or limits on what a service can reasonably provide, rather than approval of exclusion.

**Comprehension probe:** Is meeting a rule the same as meeting an obligation? What would justify an exception, and who should decide?

**Reading connections:** [EX-R06](source-evidence.md#ex-r06), [EX-R01](source-evidence.md#ex-r01)

**Compare and probe:** Separate control over support, the quantity of support, access to an activity, and compliance with a standard. Ask for an example of an independent person who uses help. For each spending claim, ask whose opportunity changes and what cost limit the reader imagined. These propositions need not be opposed.

**Risks:** Disability is heterogeneous. Do not infer incapacity from a diagnosis, dependence, or communication needs, and do not score a person's moral worth from these answers. Independent disability-led review is missing. CR-13B leaves a cost threshold unspecified and CR-13C requires distinguishing existing obligations from new discretionary spending. WCAG documents limits of web conformance only; it does not settle the normative content of CR-13D or obligations in every public service. Vietnamese translations are author drafts.

### CR-14 — Household authority, unpaid care, and gendered roles

What makes household divisions fair, what claims arise from unpaid care, and can observed gender roles identify natural preferences?

**Competing arguments and disagreements within traditions:**

- **Liberal autonomy and voluntary household specialization:** Adults may choose unequal tasks without an outsider dictating an equal division. Internal disagreement: Liberal accounts disagree about which material conditions make choice sufficiently free and what obligations survive a formally voluntary agreement.
- **Feminist political economy and care-based entitlement:** Unpaid care helps make paid earnings possible and can ground claims to income and authority. Internal disagreement: Feminist approaches disagree over wages for care, shared property, public services, labor-market participation, and whether an observed preference is adaptive.
- **Family stewardship and contribution-based authority:** Responsibility for earning and sustaining a household may be offered as a reason for differentiated decision roles. Internal disagreement: Communitarian and conservative arguments need not give the higher earner final authority; some defend mutual duties or care-based authority instead. Do not assign this lens to every endorsement.

**Reading connections:** [EX-R02](source-evidence.md#ex-r02), [EX-R11](source-evidence.md#ex-r11), [EX-R12](source-evidence.md#ex-r12)

#### CR-14A · Normative

**EN:** When adults have secure ways to live separately, a division of paid work and unpaid care that they freely agree is fair, even if one person does nearly all the care.

**VI — author draft:** Khi những người trưởng thành có điều kiện bảo đảm để sống riêng, sự phân chia việc có thu nhập và việc chăm sóc không hưởng lương mà họ tự nguyện thỏa thuận là công bằng, ngay cả khi một người làm gần như toàn bộ việc chăm sóc.

**Added distinction:** Voluntary agreement and a secure exit as sufficient grounds of fairness despite unequal tasks. This adds conditions of household consent to CR-04's duties to dependents.

**Rival reading:** Agreement may mean outsiders should not interfere rather than that the arrangement is fair. Disagreement may concern lost opportunities despite the stipulated secure exit.

**Comprehension probe:** Could an arrangement be freely chosen but unfair? What did secure living separately include?

**Reading connections:** [EX-R02](source-evidence.md#ex-r02), [EX-R11](source-evidence.md#ex-r11), [EX-R12](source-evidence.md#ex-r12)

#### CR-14B · Normative / economic

**EN:** An adult who gives up earnings to care for family members is entitled to a share of the income another adult earns during that period.

**VI — author draft:** Một người trưởng thành từ bỏ thu nhập để chăm sóc người trong gia đình có quyền được hưởng một phần thu nhập mà người trưởng thành khác kiếm được trong thời gian đó.

**Added distinction:** Care as a basis of entitlement to another household member's earnings, rather than help granted at the earner's discretion. The share and legal mechanism remain unspecified.

**Rival reading:** Agreement may presuppose a prior promise or a spouse's legal rights; disagreement may still support an independent public care income.

**Comprehension probe:** Would the claim remain without a prior promise? Does it depend on marriage, and who did you imagine earning the income?

**Reading connections:** [EX-R11](source-evidence.md#ex-r11), [EX-R02](source-evidence.md#ex-r02)

#### CR-14C · Normative / institutional

**EN:** The adult who contributes most of a household's money should have the final say in major household spending decisions.

**VI — author draft:** Người trưởng thành đóng góp phần lớn tiền của hộ gia đình nên có quyền quyết định cuối cùng về các khoản chi tiêu lớn của hộ.

**Added distinction:** Cash contribution as a basis of final authority, distinct from expertise, consultation, equal voting, or ownership of entirely personal spending money.

**Rival reading:** Agreement may be conditional on responsibility for debts; disagreement may allow separate personal accounts while rejecting authority over common resources.

**Comprehension probe:** Does earning more mean knowing more about the household's needs? What decision did you consider major?

**Reading connections:** [EX-R02](source-evidence.md#ex-r02), [EX-R11](source-evidence.md#ex-r11)

#### CR-14D · Explanatory / ontological

**EN:** A long-standing difference between women's and men's family roles is evidence of different natural inclinations, even when those roles have carried unequal rewards.

**VI — author draft:** Sự khác biệt lâu đời giữa vai trò của phụ nữ và nam giới trong gia đình là bằng chứng cho những thiên hướng tự nhiên khác nhau, ngay cả khi các vai trò ấy được hưởng lợi ích không ngang nhau.

**Added distinction:** Inference from a persistent social pattern to natural inclinations under unequal incentives. It extends CR-01 beyond how political goals are formed to what observed social arrangements can reveal about human nature.

**Rival reading:** Agreement may treat the pattern as weak evidence rather than proof. Disagreement need not deny all biological differences or individual choices. Neither answer implies approval of any role assignment.

**Comprehension probe:** How strong is the evidence? What observation could distinguish natural inclination from adaptation to rewards or restrictions?

**Reading connections:** [EX-R02](source-evidence.md#ex-r02), [EX-R11](source-evidence.md#ex-r11)

**Compare and probe:** Separate a freely chosen division of tasks from authority over common resources, a care-based claim from charity, and an explanation of gender differences from endorsement of them. Ask whether the reader assumed marriage, two genders, one culture, or a legally dependent spouse; none is a general eligibility rule for this bank.

**Risks:** These four stems do not cover gender or sexuality comprehensively. Money, free agreement, care, and a secure exit need concrete paraphrase tests. A formally available exit can still be costly; unequal earnings do not prove coercion. CR-14D intentionally raises a contested inference and does not assert that biology or social institutions alone explain gender. Independent feminist, queer, disability, and affected-reader review remains necessary. Mill's historical arguments are not a proxy for contemporary feminism; Pateman remains a reading proposal.

### CR-S07 — Who directs the additional participation support?

Role: `public_servant` · Locality: `urban` · Cluster: `CR-13`

#### English participant draft

**Surroundings.** You administer a disability-support program in a large city of your assigned fictional country. Twenty adults use additional assistance to attend community activities. Their existing assistance, access protections, and immediate safety needs are funded separately and stay unchanged. Each adult can express their choices, with communication support where needed.

**Your role and powers.** You are a non-elected service officer authorized to choose one of three approved delivery arrangements for a voluntary twelve-week extension. You cannot reduce existing support, override anyone's decisions, or compel participation. The twenty adults have agreed to enter this pilot under whichever arrangement is selected; any can decline a visit without losing existing assistance.

**What is fixed.** The extension has 120 fictional funding units each week. Quotes include travel, communication support, accessible meetings, and administration except the separately stated peer-board cost. All workers meet the same competence and safety requirements. Three qualified individual providers have appointments available. No arrangement has established superior outcomes in this city. Unspent support for someone who declines is held for that person during the pilot, not diverted to another participant.

**Decision.** Which approved arrangement would you implement? Each costs exactly 120 units per week. You may instead answer uncertain, none of these represents my decision, or skip.

**A — Individual choice of provider and time**

Give each adult a six-unit weekly allowance usable only for this support. At three units per hour, each receives two hours and chooses the provider, available appointment, and activity.

People control their own arrangements but receive fewer support hours than under the pooled packages.

**B — Pooled service with individual preferences recorded**

Contract one team at two units per hour. Each adult receives three hours. The team fixes a weekly timetable after consulting everyone; within their slot, each person chooses the activity and may refuse it.

Everyone receives the most hours, but individuals cannot change the team's fixed appointment times or choose a different provider.

**C — A user-governed pooled service**

Spend twenty units on accessible peer-board administration and one hundred on support at two units per hour: two and a half hours per adult. All twenty adults have an equal vote over the timetable and provider, with an independent route to challenge exclusion. Individuals choose their activity and can refuse visits.

Users share control but each gets half an hour less than in the staff-run pool, and a person can lose a vote about the timetable.

**Optional reason for your choice:**

- Individual control over support
- More support time within the same budget
- Shared control over the service
- Confidence in a provider or decision process
- Protection when an individual disagrees with others
- Other reason

Putting aside your responsibility as the service officer, which package do you personally prefer under the same budget, consent rules, and support needs? This is optional and may be the same choice.

#### Vietnamese participant draft

**Bối cảnh.** Bạn quản lý một chương trình hỗ trợ người khuyết tật tại một thành phố lớn của quốc gia giả định được giao. Hai mươi người trưởng thành sử dụng hỗ trợ bổ sung để tham gia hoạt động cộng đồng. Phần hỗ trợ hiện có, các bảo đảm tiếp cận và nhu cầu an toàn trước mắt của họ có kinh phí riêng và không thay đổi. Mỗi người đều có thể thể hiện lựa chọn của mình, với hỗ trợ giao tiếp khi cần.

**Vai trò và thẩm quyền.** Bạn là viên chức phụ trách dịch vụ, không giữ chức vụ dân cử, được quyền chọn một trong ba cách cung cấp đã được phê duyệt cho chương trình bổ sung tự nguyện kéo dài mười hai tuần. Bạn không được giảm hỗ trợ hiện có, quyết định thay người nhận hoặc buộc họ tham gia. Hai mươi người đã đồng ý tham gia thử nghiệm theo phương án được chọn; bất kỳ ai cũng có thể từ chối một buổi hỗ trợ mà không mất phần hỗ trợ hiện có.

**Những điều giữ nguyên.** Chương trình có 120 đơn vị kinh phí giả định mỗi tuần. Báo giá bao gồm đi lại, hỗ trợ giao tiếp, các cuộc họp dễ tiếp cận và quản lý, trừ khoản chi riêng cho ban đại diện được nêu dưới đây. Mọi nhân viên đáp ứng cùng yêu cầu năng lực và an toàn. Có ba đơn vị cung cấp riêng lẻ đủ điều kiện và còn lịch trống. Chưa phương án nào được chứng minh cho kết quả tốt hơn tại thành phố này. Phần hỗ trợ chưa dùng khi một người từ chối được giữ lại cho người đó trong thời gian thử nghiệm, không chuyển cho người khác.

**Quyết định.** Bạn sẽ triển khai cách cung cấp đã được phê duyệt nào? Mỗi cách tốn đúng 120 đơn vị mỗi tuần. Bạn cũng có thể chọn chưa chắc, không phương án nào thể hiện quyết định của tôi, hoặc bỏ qua.

**A — Từng người chọn đơn vị cung cấp và thời gian**

Cấp cho mỗi người khoản sáu đơn vị mỗi tuần chỉ dùng cho hỗ trợ này. Với giá ba đơn vị mỗi giờ, mỗi người nhận hai giờ và tự chọn đơn vị cung cấp, lịch còn trống và hoạt động.

Mỗi người tự quyết định cách hỗ trợ nhưng nhận ít giờ hơn so với các phương án gộp nguồn lực.

**B — Dịch vụ chung có ghi nhận sở thích của từng người**

Ký hợp đồng với một nhóm nhân viên giá hai đơn vị mỗi giờ. Mỗi người nhận ba giờ. Nhóm ấn định lịch hằng tuần sau khi hỏi ý kiến mọi người; trong khung giờ của mình, mỗi người chọn hoạt động và có thể từ chối.

Mọi người nhận số giờ nhiều nhất, nhưng không thể đổi khung giờ cố định của nhóm hoặc chọn đơn vị cung cấp khác.

**C — Dịch vụ chung do người sử dụng cùng quản lý**

Dùng hai mươi đơn vị cho hoạt động dễ tiếp cận của ban đại diện người sử dụng và một trăm đơn vị mua hỗ trợ với giá hai đơn vị mỗi giờ: mỗi người hai giờ rưỡi. Cả hai mươi người có quyền biểu quyết ngang nhau về lịch và đơn vị cung cấp, cùng một kênh độc lập để yêu cầu xem xét việc bị loại trừ. Từng người chọn hoạt động và có thể từ chối các buổi hỗ trợ.

Người sử dụng cùng quyết định nhưng mỗi người nhận ít hơn nửa giờ so với dịch vụ chung do nhân viên điều hành, và một người có thể thuộc phía thiểu số khi biểu quyết lịch.

**Lý do lựa chọn (không bắt buộc):**

- Quyền tự quyết cách hỗ trợ
- Có nhiều giờ hỗ trợ hơn trong cùng ngân sách
- Cùng quyết định về dịch vụ
- Tin tưởng đơn vị cung cấp hoặc quy trình quyết định
- Bảo vệ người không đồng ý với những người khác
- Lý do khác

Tạm gác trách nhiệm viên chức, cá nhân bạn thích phương án nào trong cùng điều kiện ngân sách, đồng ý tham gia và nhu cầu hỗ trợ? Câu này không bắt buộc; bạn có thể chọn giống câu trước.

#### Author review

**Limits:** No option establishes respect or disrespect for disabled people, a judgment about capacity, or a preferred welfare regime. Quantity, individual scheduling, collective voice, and expected reliability can explain the same choice. National authorization and disability-led feasibility review remain pending; the stated prices are invented scenario facts, not estimates of real services.

**Comprehension probes:**

- Who decides the activity under B?
- How many hours does each adult receive under each arrangement?
- Can anyone lose existing assistance by refusing the extension?
- Does a vote in C guarantee that every person gets their preferred time?

**Editorial variant CR-S07-V1:** Replace the fact that three qualified individual providers have appointments available with one qualified individual provider having the same appointment availability.

**Held constant:** Hold appointment availability, prices, hours, competence, safety, existing support, consent, the pooled team, and peer-board rules fixed.

**Variant limits:** An editorial contrast in provider choice, not a causal estimate of autonomy. A changed answer could reflect inferred competition or reliability; ask about those assumptions.

The alternate form and its translation are not drafted; this is a controlled-change proposal.

### CR-S08 — Who controls the additional household grant?

Role: `constituent` · Locality: `rural` · Cluster: `CR-14`

#### English participant draft

**Surroundings.** In a rural district of your assigned fictional country, a voluntary mutual-aid association is distributing an additional grant to twelve two-adult households caring for a dependent relative. The same adult does most unpaid care in each household; ten of these primary carers are women and two are men. These facts describe this group only. The other adult does some care and contributes more cash income. Existing income, essential care, and public entitlements are unchanged.

**Your role and powers.** You are an ordinary resident and association member. Your household is not eligible for this grant. You have one vote on the grant rule and cannot order anyone to change their family arrangements. A majority selects one of three approved rules. Every recipient adult has agreed to the voting procedure, understands all three rules, and may decline the new grant without losing other support.

**What is fixed.** The fund contains 120 fictional money units: ten per household under every rule. Fees and administration are paid separately. Every adult can use an individual account; the joint account requires both adults to authorize withdrawals. The grant is additional unrestricted money, not wages owed or reimbursement for an earlier expense. All arrangements are lawful in this fictional exercise. No reliable evidence shows which rule produces greater household well-being.

**Decision.** Which rule would you vote for? The vote allocates control of this grant only; it does not settle ownership of other income or change anyone's care duties. You may instead answer uncertain, none of these represents my decision, or skip.

**A — Give control to the main unpaid carer**

Pay all ten units into the primary carer's individual account. That person decides whether to spend, save, or share the grant.

This recognizes the larger care contribution and provides independent resources, but the other adult has no guaranteed control over any part of the grant.

**B — Give each adult an equal personal share**

Pay five units into each adult's individual account. Each independently controls their share.

Both adults gain independent resources, but the rule gives the larger unpaid-care contribution no extra recognition.

**C — Require a shared decision on the whole grant**

Pay ten units into the joint account. Both adults must agree to any withdrawal.

Neither adult can spend the grant unilaterally, but disagreement can leave the whole grant unused and neither gains an independently controlled share.

**Optional reason for your choice:**

- Recognition of unpaid care
- Equal individual access to resources
- Household decisions made together
- Protection from dependence on another adult's permission
- Expected practical benefit to the household
- Other reason

Apart from your responsibility as an association voter, which rule do you personally prefer with the same twelve households, amount, and consent conditions? This optional answer may match your vote.

#### Vietnamese participant draft

**Bối cảnh.** Tại một huyện nông thôn của quốc gia giả định được giao, một hội tương trợ tự nguyện cấp khoản hỗ trợ bổ sung cho mười hai hộ, mỗi hộ có hai người trưởng thành chăm sóc một người thân cần hỗ trợ. Trong mỗi hộ, cùng một người đảm nhận phần lớn việc chăm sóc không hưởng lương; mười người chăm sóc chính là phụ nữ và hai người là nam giới. Đây chỉ là đặc điểm của nhóm này. Người trưởng thành còn lại có làm một phần việc chăm sóc và đóng góp nhiều thu nhập bằng tiền hơn. Thu nhập hiện có, việc chăm sóc thiết yếu và các quyền lợi công không thay đổi.

**Vai trò và thẩm quyền.** Bạn là cư dân bình thường và thành viên của hội. Hộ của bạn không thuộc diện nhận khoản này. Bạn có một phiếu chọn quy tắc cấp tiền và không có quyền buộc ai thay đổi việc gia đình. Đa số chọn một trong ba quy tắc đã được chấp thuận. Mỗi người trưởng thành nhận hỗ trợ đã đồng ý với thủ tục biểu quyết, hiểu cả ba quy tắc và có thể từ chối khoản mới mà không mất hỗ trợ khác.

### CR-15 — Emergency authority, renewal, and local discretion

What can authorize exceptional action, who must justify its continuation, and where should decision power sit when danger and knowledge are uneven?

**Competing arguments and disagreements within traditions:**

- **Liberal constitutional arguments:** Prior authorization, enforceable limits, and contestable decisions can protect people against emergency powers that become ordinary practice. Internal disagreement: Liberal arguments also differ on executive prerogative when law cannot anticipate urgent needs; Locke's account prevents treating all discretionary action as uniquely anti-liberal. A contemporary sunset or judicial-review mechanism is an author proposal, not a quotation from Locke.
- **Protection-centered accounts of political authority:** Concentrated command can be defended as a means to provide protection and prevent conflicting decisions during danger. Internal disagreement: Hobbes's relation between protection and obedience does not establish endorsement of every leader or every restriction. His theory of sovereignty is not a theory of modern temporary emergency legislation.
- **Decentralist and dispersed-knowledge arguments:** People close to changing conditions may act on useful information before a central authority can verify it. Internal disagreement: Local discretion can also create cross-boundary costs or reproduce local domination; recognizing dispersed knowledge does not decide who should have final authority.

**Reading connections:** [EX-R03](source-evidence.md#ex-r03), [EX-R04](source-evidence.md#ex-r04), [EX-R13](source-evidence.md#ex-r13), [EX-R15](source-evidence.md#ex-r15)

#### CR-15A · Normative / political authority

**EN:** When a serious threat may spread before elected representatives can meet, a government leader can be justified in taking temporary powers that ordinary law does not grant.

**VI — author draft:** Khi một mối đe dọa nghiêm trọng có thể lan rộng trước khi các đại biểu dân cử kịp họp, việc người đứng đầu chính quyền tạm thời sử dụng những quyền hạn mà pháp luật thông thường không trao có thể là chính đáng.

**Added distinction:** Possible justification for exceptional executive discretion outside ordinary authorization, rather than approval of the exercise of existing emergency powers. Adds a timing and authorization problem beyond CR-12B's general performance-based legitimacy claim.

**Rival reading:** Agreement may assume a power narrowly necessary to protect life, enforceable later accountability, or a harmless administrative shortcut; it does not establish approval of arbitrary detention or suspension of all limits. Disagreement may doubt that a temporary power can be made temporary.

**Comprehension probe:** What exact power did you imagine? Does justified mean morally defensible, legally permitted, or excused after the event? Would the answer change if representatives could authorize it in time?

**Reading connections:** [EX-R15](source-evidence.md#ex-r15), [EX-R04](source-evidence.md#ex-r04)

#### CR-15B · Normative / political authority

**EN:** A public emergency does not by itself give a leader authority to suspend legal limits, even when waiting for authorization is likely to cause preventable harm.

**VI — author draft:** Bản thân tình trạng khẩn cấp không trao cho người lãnh đạo quyền đình chỉ các giới hạn pháp luật, ngay cả khi việc chờ được cho phép có khả năng gây ra thiệt hại có thể tránh được.

**Added distinction:** An authorization constraint despite an acknowledged cost of delay. The item asks whether the emergency itself supplies authority, not whether law should contain advance emergency procedures.

**Rival reading:** Agreement can coexist with strong advance emergency powers or a willingness to act and accept legal consequences. **Comprehension probe:** Could advance authorization solve your objection? Is a leader entitled to act, merely excused for acting, or still answerable for breaking a rule? Which legal limit did you assume?

#### CR-15C · Normative / institutional design

**EN:** Emergency restrictions should expire unless officials justify renewing them, even if renewal delays leave a temporary gap in protection.

**VI — author draft:** Các hạn chế áp dụng trong tình trạng khẩn cấp nên hết hiệu lực nếu quan chức không đưa ra được căn cứ để gia hạn, ngay cả khi việc chậm gia hạn tạo ra một khoảng trống bảo vệ tạm thời.

### CR-16 — Data consent, collective effects, and decision authority

When data about some people changes decisions affecting others, what roles should individual permission, affected communities, independent oversight, and human reconsideration have?

**Competing arguments and disagreements within traditions:**

- **Individual-rights and contractual arguments:** Informed permission and meaningful control over one's own records can protect personal independence and voluntary exchange. Internal disagreement: Individual-rights arguments differ over whether a person's permission can authorize effects on others, whether consent under unequal bargaining power is meaningful, and whether some uses should be prohibited regardless of consent.
- **Democratic and republican arguments:** People affected by classifications or group-level inferences can claim a say over rules even if they supplied no records themselves. Internal disagreement: Collective governance can constrain organizations while empowering majorities over dissenters; a vote, an individual remedy, and a right of exit are different protections.
- **Public-benefit and expertise-based arguments:** Purpose-bound analysis and accountable expertise can support useful coordination while reducing some burdens of repeated permission requests. Internal disagreement: Claims of public benefit do not by themselves settle who defines the purpose, which harms count, or how an affected person can obtain a remedy. High average accuracy can coexist with concentrated errors.

**Reading connections:** [EX-R05](source-evidence.md#ex-r05), [EX-R06](source-evidence.md#ex-r06)

#### CR-16A · Normative / authorization

**EN:** An adult's informed permission is enough to authorize use of their data, even when patterns inferred from it influence decisions about people who did not share data.

**Added distinction:** Whether permission from the data contributor is sufficient when inference reaches non-contributors. This concerns the reach of authorization, not whether consent is unnecessary or whether every inference is harmful.

**Rival reading:** Agreement may assume only beneficial or harmless group effects, additional background protections, or that the statement concerns collection alone. **Comprehension probe:** Who has given permission, and who may be affected? Does your answer concern collecting the original record or making a later decision about someone else?

**VI — author draft:** Người chịu tác động của một quyết định tự động về dịch vụ công nên có quyền yêu cầu con người xem xét lại theo cùng quy tắc đủ điều kiện, ngay cả khi hệ thống tự động nhìn chung mắc ít sai sót hơn.

### CR-S09 — Who can close a route before the flood arrives?

Role: `national_leader` · Locality: `urban` · Cluster: `CR-15`

#### English participant draft

**Your surroundings.** Three neighboring urban districts in your assigned fictional country share river crossings. A flood forecast estimates a one-in-three chance that ordinary roads beside the river will become unsafe within six hours. The same forecast and road sensors are available under every plan. A mistaken closure can delay paid work and deliveries; a late closure can put road users in danger. No plan's total harm is known in advance.

**Your role and authority.** You are the national leader responsible for choosing one of three coordination arrangements already authorized by the legislature. A temporary law permits closures of the specified ordinary roads for the next 48 hours. You cannot extend that period, order detention, compel evacuation, or change ordinary rights outside these road powers. Emergency vehicles have protected routes under all plans.

**What is fixed.** Each arrangement uses the same 30 trained staff and the same approved budget of 200 fictional credits. Sensors, public warnings, transport assistance, compensation rules, written reasons for closures, and access to a binding review within 24 hours are identical. All staff can communicate with district teams. The differences below concern who may authorize a closure and when an additional review occurs. Closing a road is not a judgment that every person in the district must leave.

**Which arrangement would you authorize for the 48-hour period?** Choose A, B, or C; you may instead answer uncertain, none of these represents my decision, or skip. The forecasts and costs are exercise facts, not claims about any real country.

**A — One command, followed by review**

A national coordinator can immediately close a specified road after receiving sensor data and district reports. An independent panel reviews each closure within six hours and can end it. District teams implement the coordinator's decisions.

Decisions can be coordinated across boundaries without waiting for prior panel approval. A mistaken closure can operate for up to six hours before that panel reviews it, and district teams cannot independently depart from the decision.

**B — District decisions with a boundary referee**

Each district's designated officer can immediately close specified roads inside that district for up to two hours at a time, using local reports as well as sensors. A national referee resolves conflicting cross-boundary instructions within 45 minutes and can reverse a closure.

Local officers can act on details that the center has not verified. Conflicting directions at a shared crossing can remain in force for up to 45 minutes; neighboring users may receive incompatible advice during that interval.

**C — Independent approval before a closure**

The national coordinator proposes closures using the same reports and sensors. A standing independent panel can approve, narrow, or reject each proposal before it takes effect; each review takes 30 minutes. The coordinator then issues one instruction across the affected districts.

An independent body checks the scope before people are restricted. The same 30-minute wait applies to a well-founded urgent proposal, during which the proposed closure cannot begin.

**Optional reason for your choice:**

- Fast and consistent action across districts
- Use of knowledge available to local officers
- An independent check before a restriction starts
- A workable way to correct a mistaken closure
- The likely balance between road danger and disruption
- Other reason

Optional: setting aside your assigned responsibility as national leader, which of these same arrangements would you personally prefer under the same forecast, powers, resources, and consequences? You may be uncertain, find none representative, or skip. This does not change your role decision.

### CR-S10 — Who decides what the travel records may reveal?

#### English participant draft

**Your surroundings.** A civil society coalition in a large city of your assigned fictional country holds travel diaries volunteered by 1,200 adults from six neighborhoods. The city will use aggregate analyses to plan the locations and times of a fixed number of evening bus services. Decisions can benefit or inconvenience residents who supplied no diary. The records describe past trips; they do not reveal everyone's future needs.

**Your role and authority.** You lead the coalition. Its members have authorized you to choose one of three governance packages for the next twelve weeks. A written agreement makes your chosen package binding on the coalition's analysts and the city's use of the supplied analysis. You cannot add bus funding, compel anyone to provide records, change city law, or publish identifiable trips.

**What is fixed.** Each package costs the full 240-credit administration grant. The same trained analysts, secure storage, accessible paper and telephone participation routes, and technical checks apply. Existing permission covers the stated transport-planning use; in every package, contributors may withdraw their records from future analyses without penalty. No employer, police body, or commercial advertiser may obtain the records. Records are deleted after twelve weeks. The bus budget, legal protections, and prohibition on individual eligibility decisions are identical. The additional governance choices below do not remove these baseline protections.

**Which package would you choose?** Choose A, B, or C; you may instead answer uncertain, none of these represents my decision, or skip. A package's predicted effect on data quality and trust is uncertain. The exercise does not ask you to endorse data collection generally.

**A — Ask contributors before each analysis**

Send an accessible description of each proposed transport analysis to every contributor. Use only records whose contributors give fresh permission for that analysis. The grant funds explanations and follow-up through online, paper, and telephone routes.

Contributors gain control over each use of their records. Non-contributors receive no additional say over analyses that may affect their neighborhood, and low response to a request can leave some travel patterns poorly represented.

**B — Let a neighborhood panel authorize analyses**

Fund a 12-person panel selected by a public lottery from willing adult residents, with two seats per neighborhood and participation support. Contributors and non-contributors are both eligible. By majority vote, the panel may approve, narrow, or refuse an analysis within the agreed transport purpose; it cannot override an individual's withdrawal or baseline protections.

People affected without supplying records gain a possible route into decisions. A resident may not be selected or may lose a vote, and the panel can block an analysis that other residents or analysts consider useful.

**C — Use an independent steward with binding review**

Fund an independent data steward to check every proposed analysis against the agreed purpose, disclosure risk, and equal-treatment rules. Any resident can ask for a review without charge. The steward can require changes or stop an analysis, must explain decisions publicly without revealing personal data, and has no financial interest in a chosen bus route.

Contributors need not answer every new request, and all residents can challenge a proposed analysis. The steward applies the existing rules; residents gain neither a fresh individual approval of every use nor a collective vote to reject an otherwise permitted analysis.

**Optional reason for your choice:**

- Control by the people whose records are used
- A voice for residents affected without supplying records
- Enforceable limits applied by an independent reviewer
- Useful analysis with manageable participation demands
- Protection for a person who disagrees with a group decision
- Other reason

Optional: setting aside your responsibility as the coalition's leader, which of these same packages would you personally prefer under the same data permissions, budget, bus plans, protections, and consequences? You may be uncertain, find none representative, or skip. Your role decision remains separately recorded.

### CR-17 — Authority over shared resources and membership within self-government

Who should have standing to govern a shared resource when established users, newer users, affected non-users and a wider public have different claims? This extends CR-08C beyond recognition of collective self-rule to its internal decision rules; it does not define who constitutes a people.

**Competing arguments and disagreements within traditions:**

- **Decentralized and polycentric governance proposals:** People who use and maintain a resource can possess relevant knowledge and sustain shared rules without a single distant manager. Internal disagreement: Decentralization alone does not settle who may join, whose rules bind outsiders, or how failures are corrected; a local board can concentrate power.
- **Liberal and republican concerns about equal standing:** New users need ways to contest inherited authority, while everyone subject to a binding rule can claim a voice in it. Internal disagreement: Equal individual voting, representative review and a wider public mandate are different remedies; none automatically settles a collective self-government claim.
- **Communitarian and stewardship arguments:** Past maintenance can create responsibilities and claims to continuity that are not exhausted by present consumption. ### CR-18 — Insecure work, shared risk and collective bargaining

How should demand risk, control over work and collective obligations be divided when people can choose hours but another organization sets payment terms? This extends CR-05 beyond enterprise decision rights to risk-bearing, bargaining exit and the costs of solidarity.

**Competing arguments and disagreements within traditions:**

- **Contractual freedom and entrepreneurial responsibility arguments:** Freedom to choose assignments and negotiate individually can support claims to keep rewards and bear corresponding risks. Internal disagreement: Formal contractual choice may coexist with unequal control over price and weak alternatives; market-oriented accounts differ over insurance, competition rules and collective organization.
- **Social-democratic and labour-protection arguments:** Organizations controlling payment conditions can be asked to share demand risk, while collective bargaining can offset dependence. Internal disagreement: Regulation, social insurance and bargaining are different institutions; protecting worker autonomy can conflict with making a collective agreement universal.
- **Socialist and mutual-aid arguments:** Workers can build shared capacity and support others even when action brings an immediate personal cost. Internal disagreement: Solidarity need not require compulsory membership, identical priorities or control by one representative body; relief and bargaining can complement each other.
- **Capability and effective-choice questions:** Practical alternatives affect the meaning of consent and exit even when no one is formally compelled to work. Internal disagreement: A concern for effective choice does not select one labour classification, ownership form or intervention, and outcomes depend on circumstances.

### CR-19 — Production, distribution and economic coordination

How should people understand productive capacity, contributions, reinvestment and control of the surplus generated by shared work?

**Competing arguments and disagreements within traditions:**

- **Marxist and socialist accounts:** Production depends on relations among workers and control over resources; distribution of output may reflect bargaining and property rather than contribution alone. Internal disagreement: Socialists disagree over markets, planning, ownership and whether labor alone explains prices.
- **Liberal and market institutional accounts:** Savings, risk and decentralized coordination can enable productive investment and generate a claim to returns. Internal disagreement: Liberals disagree about bargaining power, inheritance, corrective taxation and the just share of returns.
- **Cooperative and social-reproduction accounts:** Shared productive capacity also depends on maintenance and unpaid caring activities that market receipts do not fully capture. Internal disagreement: Support for recognizing care does not determine which family, employer or public institution should provide it.

### CR-20 — Material organization, institutions and cultural change

When economic arrangements, laws, and shared beliefs change, which can influence the others, and how much independence can each retain?

**Competing arguments and disagreements within traditions:**

- **Historical materialist approaches:** Property, work and production can shape institutions and beliefs that help sustain an economic order. Internal disagreement: The degree of autonomy of law and culture and the direction of influence remain contested among historical materialists.
- **Institutionalist and interpretive approaches:** Rules, collective ideas and organizations can alter incentives and economic behavior rather than simply reflecting production. Internal disagreement: Accounts differ over the causal weight of formal rules, economic resources and cultural practices.
- **Pluralist and conservative approaches:** Traditions and organized beliefs may persist through economic change and may have meanings beyond immediate material interests. Internal disagreement: Persistence does not show that a tradition is fair, and traditions can also change through conflict and economic pressure.

### CR-21 — Social entities, collective agency, and relations

What kinds of things exist in social explanation: continuing institutions, individual actors, organized collective goals, and relations that constitute positions?

**Competing arguments and disagreements within traditions:**

- **Methodological individualism and interpretive sociology:** Accounts of institutions should remain connected to people's actions and the meanings actors attach to them. Internal disagreement: Explaining through individuals does not require denying institutions, social relationships, or the causal effects of rules; Weber is a reading proposal, not an endorsement label.
- **Relational and historical-materialist approaches:** Ownership, labor, and dependence can constitute positions even when people do not identify with those positions. Internal disagreement: These approaches disagree about causal primacy, the autonomy of politics and culture, and how collective organization emerges from a shared position.
- **Institutional and emergent accounts of collective agency:** Rules and relations can sustain commitments and patterns that outlive particular officeholders or differ from members' personal wishes. Internal disagreement: Accepting collective action does not settle whether organizations literally have minds, who may represent them, or whether their actions are legitimate.

### CR-22 — What warrants political knowledge under uncertainty?

What makes an expectation or explanation credible, how should informed disagreement change confidence, and where do empirical findings stop short of choosing political ends?

**Competing arguments and disagreements within traditions:**

- **Predictive and causal approaches to social knowledge:** Stable patterns can guide expectations, while causal claims ask what would change under a different intervention or condition. Internal disagreement: Predictive usefulness need not establish an intervention effect; a well-supported causal claim may still leave its mechanism uncertain. Requiring a full mechanism for every practical decision can also demand more than the available evidence permits.
- **Conciliatory and steadfast responses to informed disagreement:** Disagreement by a comparably informed researcher can be evidence about the limits of one’s own judgment. Internal disagreement: Researchers disagree on how much confidence to reduce and when apparently equal expertise conceals relevant differences. Treating peers seriously does not require averaging every political opinion or giving uninformed denial equal weight.
- **Interpretive and value-conscious social inquiry:** An account can clarify meanings, relations, or patterned exclusion without predicting every individual outcome; choices about which consequences matter also require evaluative commitments. Internal disagreement: Interpretation still needs evidence and exposure to rival accounts. Acknowledging values does not make all factual claims equally credible, and empirical findings can reveal consequences that cause people to reconsider their values.

**Reading connections:** [EX-R17](source-evidence.md#ex-r17), [EX-R19](source-evidence.md#ex-r19), [EX-R20](source-evidence.md#ex-r20)

#### CR-22A · Epistemic / grounds for expectation

**EN:** A reliable pattern in social data is enough to justify expecting that pattern to continue, even when its cause is not understood.

**VI — author draft:** Một xu hướng lặp lại đáng tin cậy trong dữ liệu xã hội là đủ căn cứ để kỳ vọng xu hướng đó tiếp tục, ngay cả khi chưa hiểu nguyên nhân của nó.

**Added distinction:** Predictive warrant without an understood causal process. This is not a claim that changing a variable used by a predictive model will change the outcome, or that prediction should determine political priorities.

**Rival reading:** Agreement may assume stable conditions, while disagreement may anticipate changes in institutions or behavior. Readers may use reliable to mean already causally established, making the proposition circular. Neither answer demonstrates statistical competence.

**Comprehension probe:** What makes the pattern reliable? Would your answer change if officials acted on it and thereby changed the conditions that produced it? Does expecting an outcome tell us how to cause a different one?

**Reading connections:** [EX-R19](source-evidence.md#ex-r19)

#### CR-22B · Epistemic / disagreement

**EN:** When equally well-informed researchers reach opposite conclusions from the same evidence, each should lower their confidence even if neither finds an error in their own reasoning.

**VI — author draft:** Khi các nhà nghiên cứu có mức độ nắm thông tin ngang nhau đi đến kết luận trái ngược từ cùng bằng chứng, mỗi người nên giảm mức độ chắc chắn của mình, ngay cả khi không ai tìm thấy lỗi trong lập luận của bản thân.

**Added distinction:** The evidential significance of disagreement under an explicit peer condition. This differs from choosing an expert over a layperson or assigning equal weight to every public claim.

**Rival reading:** Agreement may call only for a small confidence adjustment; disagreement may concern hidden differences in assumptions or the possibility that one conclusion is much better supported. Readers may refuse the stipulated equality of information rather than endorse steadfastness.

**Comprehension probe:** How much would confidence change, and why? Are you assuming one researcher has information or a relevant skill that the other lacks? Would lowering confidence require abandoning the conclusion?

**Reading connections:** [EX-R20](source-evidence.md#ex-r20)

#### CR-22C · Epistemic / explanation and prediction

**EN:** An explanation of a pattern of social exclusion can count as knowledge even if it cannot predict precisely which individual will be excluded next.

**VI — author draft:** Một cách giải thích xu hướng loại trừ lặp lại trong xã hội có thể được xem là tri thức, ngay cả khi không dự đoán chính xác cá nhân nào sẽ bị loại trừ tiếp theo.

#### CR-22D · Epistemic / facts and evaluation

**EN:** Evidence about a policy’s consequences cannot by itself settle which of those consequences should matter most.

**VI — author draft:** Chỉ riêng bằng chứng về hệ quả của một chính sách không thể quyết định hệ quả nào nên được coi trọng nhất.

### CR-S16 — What would you act on when the evidence leaves two explanations?

#### English participant draft

**Your surroundings.** You administer appointments for an existing public-support service in a large city of your assigned fictional country. Some residents miss appointments and must wait for a later date. Across six neighborhoods, records show higher attendance among residents who received a reminder call. Calls were not assigned by lottery: staff first called people who were easiest to reach. The records do not establish what would have happened to the same residents without a call.

Two researchers with equal access to the records and comparable relevant expertise disagree. One thinks reminders account for much of the difference; the other thinks reliable phone access and predictable working hours account for much of it. These accounts identify possible mechanisms but do not establish how common each is.

**Your role and authority.** You are a non-elected service officer authorized to choose one approved support package for the next two weeks. You are not being asked to certify which explanation is true. You cannot change eligibility, add money, make participation compulsory, or penalize someone for declining help. The choice must be made before stronger evidence can be collected.

**What is fixed.** Each package uses exactly 40 additional staff-hours. Existing appointment hours, ordinary reminders, accessible booking, assistance, eligibility, and appeal rules continue under every package. The additional offers below go to residents with appointments due during the two weeks; nobody loses existing support. Within each package, a public lottery allocates offers if demand exceeds the stated capacity. The number of missed appointments prevented by each package is unknown. More offers do not guarantee more completed appointments.

**Which package would you authorize now?** Choose A, B, or C; you may instead answer uncertain, none of these represents my decision, or skip. Keep your reason for acting separate from your confidence about what caused the earlier attendance difference.

**A — Offer additional reminder calls**

Offer 80 residents an additional 30-minute staff contact, including attempts to reach them and an accessible explanation of the appointment. Staff can confirm or rearrange the booking within existing service hours.

The package reaches the largest number of residents and acts on the observed association. It adds no evening appointments or individual travel planning, and the earlier association may partly reflect who was easy to contact.

**B — Offer evening appointments**

Offer 40 additional one-hour evening appointments. The same service and eligibility rules apply. Existing daytime appointments remain available; accepting an evening slot is optional.

The package directly changes a working-hours barrier reported by some residents. It reaches fewer people than extra calls and offers no additional help with travel or remembering the date.

**C — Offer individual appointment planning**

Offer 20 residents two staff-hours each to identify their barriers and make a practical plan using existing booking and support services. Contact, accessible communication, and any staff travel must fit within those two hours; no new travel subsidy is available.

The package can respond to different explanations rather than choosing one common remedy. It reaches the fewest residents, and identifying a barrier does not guarantee the existing services can remove it.

**Optional reason for your choice:**

- The observed pattern seems useful for predicting benefit
- A proposed mechanism fits the barriers residents describe
- A flexible response is preferable while informed researchers disagree
- Reaching more residents with the available hours
- Providing more help per person despite reaching fewer people
- Other reason

Optional: setting aside your assigned responsibility as service officer, which of these same packages would you personally prefer under the same evidence, hours, service rules, and consequences? You may be uncertain, find none representative, or skip. This is separate from both your role action and your view of the causal explanation.

### CR-23 — How to investigate public problems: comparison, meaning, history and participation

Which kinds of inquiry should guide public judgment, what should they explain, and who should help shape the questions? This distinguishes a preferred research approach from beliefs about what exists (ontology), what can be known (epistemology), technical research competence and a favoured political conclusion.

**Competing arguments and disagreements within traditions:**

- **Comparative and causal-inference approaches:** Differences over time or between otherwise comparable settings can help assess whether an intervention contributed to an outcome, beyond simple reports of improvement. Internal disagreement: Design assumptions, outcomes, mechanisms and applicability remain disputed. Quantitative comparison is not synonymous with randomized experimentation and can be combined with qualitative inquiry.
- **Interpretive approaches:** Understanding what actions and institutions mean to the people involved can reveal consequences and social relationships missed by externally defined indicators. Internal disagreement: Interpretation does not make all accounts equally accurate or erase conflict within a group; researchers disagree about how to justify and challenge an interpretation.
- **Historical and structural explanation:** Tracing the formation of institutions can explain why present options, interests and inequalities take their current form. Internal disagreement: History does not establish one economic cause or determine the future; arguments differ over contingency, ideas, institutions, conflict and the scale of explanation.
- **Participatory and action-oriented inquiry:** Affected people can contribute knowledge and challenge the authority to define a problem, its evidence and useful action. Internal disagreement: Participation can reproduce unequal voice, and shared authority does not guarantee consensus, accurate findings or a particular emancipatory outcome. Transparent comparison and technical expertise can still be part of the inquiry.

#### CR-23D · Methodological / normative

**EN:** People affected by a study should be able to change its research questions, even if this reduces the researchers’ control over a fixed plan.

**Added distinction:** Authority to shape inquiry beyond being interviewed or asked to approve findings; a procedural commitment with a design tradeoff.

**Rival reading:** Agreement may refer only to a right to withdraw or correct factual errors. Disagreement may concern retrospective outcome switching, sponsor accountability or domination by vocal participants rather than rejecting all participation.
