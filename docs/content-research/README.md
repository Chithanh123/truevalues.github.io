# Content research: a denser, more revealing assessment

**Prepared 23 October; revised 25 October 2026 · Content preparation only · Author draft**

The next version should distinguish **what a person believes society is like, what they think is owed to others, which institutions they trust, and what they choose within an assigned responsibility**. The immediate priority is to replace repetition and vague propositions with questions that separate those judgments. A larger research bank can support shorter participant forms.

This pack reviews instrument `0.4.0` at commit `d0252e7` and prepares new material for review. The live question bank, code, scoring, participant route, and translations have not changed. There are no new participant findings or completed independent human reviews.

## The most important content decisions

1. **Separate economic questions.** Public financing, public ownership, equal access, consumer choice, worker power, and limits on private dependency can coexist in different combinations. The current market/public-provider axis compresses these differences. A cooperative should receive its own institutional description rather than an assumed moderate position.
2. **Separate freedom, power, and accountability.** Protection from actual interference, the practical ability to act, and protection from another person's uncontrolled power are distinct concerns. Transparency also deserves its own rationale: the current general statements about records should receive the same scoring caution as the already descriptive public-records scenario.
3. **Replace repeated cultural questions with distinct obligations.** Preserving a program, including previously excluded people, accepting inherited obligations, deferring to religious authority, and changing unjust institutions are different disputes. An answer about a festival cannot establish a general position on all social change.
4. **Distinguish membership, aid, and cooperation.** A person can favor cross-border help, reject supranational authority, and prioritize a government's special responsibility to its residents. Shared practical benefits and duties to people abroad require separate evidence.
5. **Connect assumptions to mechanisms.** General claims about agency, class, care, ecological dependence, and knowledge need situations that reveal why the claim matters. Specify who can act, which consequences are known, and which fact would change the decision. Record rival reasons for the same action.

These are recommendations for a later content version. Existing response meanings must not be silently reinterpreted. Keep current value questions as a pool of possible anchors while auditing them; the new drafts do not automatically replace the entire bank.

## Twelve disputes to develop

Each cluster contains three propositions. They are separate claims that can sometimes be accepted together, not three ordered ideological positions or a new score axis. Political-tradition labels belong in author review, withheld from the participant task.

| Cluster | Central question | Why it adds useful detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CR-01 · Agency and social formation | How do stated reasons, social position, and institutions help explain political commitments? | Separates attributed interests, people's own explanations, and the formation of goals; it does not directly test the ability to dissent. |
| CR-02 · Sources of power | How do ownership, public authority, and collective organization shape dependence? | Allows concern about private concentration and state concentration together. |
| CR-03 · Freedom | Does freedom require non-interference, practical opportunity, protection against arbitrary power, or a combination? | Identical support for a protection can rest on different accounts of liberty. |
| CR-04 · Desert, need, and care | Which claims justify allocating scarce support? | Distinguishes contribution, disadvantage, and dependency without equating care with family duty. |
| CR-05 · Enterprise authority | Who should control decisions affecting investors, workers, and users? | Makes ownership and decision rights visible separately from government spending. |
| CR-06 · Knowledge | How should general evidence, situated experience, and dispersed information guide decisions? | Distinguishes knowing about a problem from being entitled to govern it. |
| CR-07 · Continuity and reform | What weight should inherited practice and transition risks have when harm continues? | Separates attachment to tradition from concern for service continuity. |
| CR-08 · Membership | What grounds a claim to participation or shared resources? | Examines residence, membership, and wider obligation without treating country as ideology. |
| CR-09 · Religion and justification | What reasons may justify rules binding people with different beliefs? | Separates personal religious commitment from public coercion and equal status. |
| CR-10 · Inherited status and repair | When do equal rules fail to remove durable exclusion? | Introduces structural barriers and historical responsibility without collapsing caste, race, and class. |
| CR-11 · Ecological standing | Who or what counts beyond the interests of present decision makers? | Distinguishes human benefit, future obligations, and nonhuman moral standing. |
| CR-12 · Hierarchy and coordination | What justifies authority, and how should common action be organized? | Examines performance, revocability, and voluntary coordination as different grounds for institutional judgment. |

The coverage is broader but remains selective. New themes require dedicated work on specific Indigenous and Black political thought, non-European traditions, disability justice, gender and sexuality, security and emergency authority, and coercive or authoritarian political arguments. The dossier provides some starting readings; a named tradition is not evidence that it has been represented adequately.

## What a more revealing scenario does

Consider a resident whose landlord has always treated tenants fairly but retains a power to end a tenancy without giving reasons. A choice to establish review could reflect fear of future mistreatment, a view that uncontrolled power already reduces freedom, or a preference for predictable investment and household planning. These are different explanations of the same action.

The scenario should make the authority, available alternatives, appeal rights, cost, and housing conditions explicit. Its follow-up should allow those different reasons. A carefully controlled variant can then change one relevant condition, such as the availability of an alternative tenancy, while holding the other facts fixed. Even then, a changed response is not by itself a measure of ideological inconsistency or a causal role effect.

The six drafted families examine housing governance, respite care, contested evidence, community membership, ecological burdens, and institutional exclusion. Each includes a feasible decision within its assigned role. They complement the current dissent and international-obligation material; form selection must preserve those concerns where they are part of the intended assessment. Six new scenarios alone are not comprehensive political coverage.

The evidence-method family, **CR-S03**, is held as a comprehension probe pending redesign. Its stated research mandate can make one method inappropriate before political priorities enter the choice. It remains useful in an interview to uncover that distinction, but should not become an ideological indicator in its current form.

Country correspondences stay out of participant wording until the final debrief. Give readers the institutions and constraints needed to decide. Withholding an explicit real-country label cannot prevent recognition or deliberate inspection of public researcher material.

## Density and participant burden

Use **three content-interview packets**, each with **12 statements plus two scenarios: 14 primary tasks**. Across the packet set, reviewers and readers can examine all 36 candidates and all six families. Briefing, reasons, probes, language switching, and the debrief add burden; no completion-time claim is established.

A later assessment could investigate a budget of **18 statements plus six scenarios**, with common anchors and rotating content. That is a design budget, not a frozen form or validated length. Select anchors after review, preserve important existing topics, and do not claim different forms provide equivalent individual scores merely because they share questions. Do not append all 36 new statements to the current 49-item route.

Evaluate density through concrete editorial questions: what does this item add, what alternative explanation survives, and which follow-up could distinguish it? Statistical information, reliability, dimensionality, language equivalence, and population comparisons require later data and a specified analysis. More disagreement is not automatically better content, and broad agreement does not automatically make a principle dispensable.

## Sequence before implementation

1. **Resolve the concept and source record.** Verify the priority readings and passages, identify rival interpretations, and select the disputes the next form will actually cover.
2. **Review the selected drafts independently.** Seek knowledgeable interpreters and critics across relevant approaches. Reconcile the English–Vietnamese wording and document what remains disputed. Generated text and author review do not replace these functions.
3. **Use targeted comprehension rounds.** Apply the three packets in stages, inspecting whether people distinguish description from obligation, authority from influence, and uncertainty from neutrality. Revise from observed problems and recheck changed wording.
4. **Freeze a content decision record.** List selected/rejected IDs, exact wording in each language, scenario versions, powers, invariant facts, response categories, form coverage, and descriptive feedback. Justify any proposed aggregation separately.
5. **Then implement the reviewed content.** Version any meaning changes, keep historical responses interpretable, and preserve the end-only country reveal.
