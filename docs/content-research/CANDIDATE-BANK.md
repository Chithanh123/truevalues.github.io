# Candidate bank: political assumptions and commitments

Content preparation draft, **23 October 2026; revised 25 October 2026**. This document proposes **36 new candidate statements in 12 debate clusters** for review before any implementation. It changes neither instrument `0.4.0` nor its 49-item route. It is a bank from which to select, revise, or reject items; it is not a proposal to make every participant answer another 36 statements.

English and Vietnamese wording below is newly authored and **unreviewed**. No item is copied from a validated scale, no empirical discrimination is demonstrated, and no tradition is asserted to endorse a statement. The [source dossier](SOURCE-DOSSIER.md) identifies proposed readings and their verification status. A conceptual connection is not a verified attribution to an author. Independent source checking, substantive criticism, bilingual reconciliation, and comprehension interviews remain necessary.

## How to use the bank

“Density” means each item adds an interpretable distinction, not that it contains more concepts or difficult vocabulary. The clusters compare different explanations or commitments around a shared question. They are **not scales, mutually exclusive alternatives, opposing endpoints, or answer keys**. Someone can reasonably agree with all three items in a cluster, or reject all three. Disagreement is not evidence for the opposite proposition.

Researcher labels, claim types, tradition associations, and the notes below should remain outside participant wording. Only the EN or VI statement is a candidate prompt. The IDs are editorial identifiers, not replacement IDs for the implemented bank. Preserve old IDs and version history separately if any candidate is eventually adopted.

The claim types are:

- **Explanatory / ontological:** what forms interests, causes outcomes, or constitutes social power. These claims may depend on which society the respondent imagines.
- **Normative:** what is owed, deserved, legitimate, or valuable. An empirical finding alone cannot establish such a claim.
- **Epistemic:** what provides grounds for a judgment and how conflicting evidence should be treated.
- **Institutional:** how authority, ownership, or coordination ought to be arranged. These usually combine values with expectations about consequences; the notes identify that bridge.

For initial interviews, record ordinary agreement, uncertainty, refusal/skip, and any volunteered condition separately. “I agree if an independent appeal exists” must not be rewritten as unconditional agreement. Ask what respondents understood before explaining the intended distinction. Use short packets containing complete clusters, as proposed in the [review protocol](REVIEW-PROTOCOL.md), rather than a compulsory full-bank interview. No numeric interpretation, tradition matching, or aggregate premise score is proposed.

## CR-01 — Agency, interests, and social formation

**Question:** Should political commitments be explained through people's own reasons, their social position, or institutions that help form their goals? Relevant debates include liberal accounts of agency, Marxist accounts of interests, communitarian accounts of social formation, and feminist relational accounts. Their internal differences are part of the question; the labels do not assign answers.

### CR-01A · Explanatory / epistemic

**EN:** A person's economic position is usually a better guide to their political interests than the goals they say they have.

**VI:** Vị trí kinh tế của một người thường là căn cứ tốt hơn để hiểu lợi ích chính trị của họ so với những mục tiêu mà chính họ nói mình theo đuổi.

**Distinction:** Objective interests attributed from a position versus the person's account of their interests. Agreement does not establish that economic position determines every belief, or that the respondent supports a particular class politics.

### CR-01B · Explanatory / epistemic

**EN:** Political commitments are best explained by the reasons people give, even when their group memberships suggest a different explanation.

**VI:** Cách tốt nhất để giải thích những lập trường chính trị mà con người theo đuổi là dựa vào các lý do họ đưa ra, ngay cả khi việc họ thuộc những nhóm xã hội nào gợi ra một cách giải thích khác.

**Distinction:** First-person reasons as explanations versus explanation by social location. Disagreement might concern rationalization, limited self-knowledge, or unreliable reporting; it does not establish that agency is unreal.

### CR-01C · Explanatory / ontological

**EN:** Institutions such as schools and workplaces help form people's political goals, rather than simply responding to goals people already have.

**VI:** Các thiết chế như trường học và nơi làm việc góp phần định hình mục tiêu chính trị của con người, chứ không chỉ đáp ứng những mục tiêu mà con người đã có sẵn.

**Distinction:** Institutions as producers of goals versus instruments for prior goals. Agreement does not identify which institutions matter or deny that people can change them.

**Contrast and probe:** A person can think interests are socially formed while trusting the reasons people give for acting on them. Ask: “What is a political interest, as distinct from a goal? Describe someone whose stated goal differs from what benefits them. What would count against your explanation?”

**Main risks:** “Interests” may mean material welfare, identity, rights, or preferences. CR-01C's examples can elicit simple agreement that education influences people; retain it only if the explanation distinguishes formation of goals from assistance in pursuing them. CR-01A may be heard as permission to dismiss other people's views, while CR-01B may be heard as courtesy rather than a claim about explanation. Vietnamese *lợi ích*, *lập trường*, and *thiết chế* need paraphrase testing. These items are demanding candidates for interviews, not introductory warm-up questions.

## CR-02 — Economic power, organized groups, and the state

**Question:** What keeps political power unequal over time? Relevant debates include Marxist class analysis, liberal pluralism, republican accounts of dependence, and theories of state autonomy. This cluster separates disproportionate influence, control of policy, and the independent interests of officials.

### CR-02A · Explanatory

**EN:** When elections are competitive, control of major businesses still gives owners political power that ordinary voting cannot offset.

**VI:** Ngay cả khi bầu cử có sự cạnh tranh, quyền kiểm soát các doanh nghiệp lớn vẫn đem lại cho chủ sở hữu quyền lực chính trị mà việc bỏ phiếu thông thường không thể cân bằng được.

**Distinction:** Durable power beyond formal electoral participation. Agreement does not establish that every business owner acts together, or that abolishing private ownership is the desired remedy.

### CR-02B · Explanatory

**EN:** The main direction of public policy usually reflects bargaining among several groups rather than the rule of one economic class.

**VI:** Định hướng chính của chính sách công thường phản ánh sự thương lượng giữa nhiều nhóm hơn là sự chi phối của một giai cấp kinh tế.

**Distinction:** Negotiated plural influence versus predominant class rule. Agreement does not establish that bargaining power is equal, fair, or open to everyone.

### CR-02C · Explanatory

**EN:** Government officials can build lasting power that serves their own interests independently of business owners.

**VI:** Quan chức nhà nước có thể xây dựng quyền lực bền vững phục vụ lợi ích riêng của họ mà không phụ thuộc vào chủ doanh nghiệp.

**Distinction:** State autonomy from owners of capital. Agreement does not show approval of bureaucracy, support for authoritarian rule, or rejection of class explanations in other cases.

**Contrast and probe:** Ask for an institution or mechanism behind each answer: ownership, investment decisions, organized labor, appointment powers, control of information, or something else. Then ask whether the claim describes the respondent's country, most states, or a possible state. CR-02A and CR-02B can coexist if unequal businesses remain one among several influential groups.

**Main risks:** Country-specific beliefs can dominate this cluster. “Several groups” may mean fractions of the same class; “independently” may mean complete isolation rather than a partly autonomous power base. CR-02C's possibility wording may attract broad assent; keep it only if interviews show that the autonomy distinction adds information beyond CR-02A/B. These are causal beliefs, not a test of whether respondents prefer inequality.

## CR-03 — What makes a choice free?

**Question:** Is freedom primarily absence of threats, practical opportunity, or protection from another person's uncontrolled power? Relevant debates include classical and social liberalism, libertarian approaches, republicanism, socialist critiques of dependence, and feminist accounts of domination. These accounts can overlap.

### CR-03A · Normative / conceptual

**EN:** An adult who accepts a job without being threatened has made a free choice, even when no other employer is hiring.

**VI:** Một người trưởng thành nhận một công việc mà không bị đe dọa đã đưa ra một lựa chọn tự do, ngay cả khi không có nơi nào khác đang tuyển dụng.

**Distinction:** Voluntary acceptance under constrained alternatives. Agreement does not establish that the wage is fair, the contract is legitimate in every respect, or the employer owes no duties.

### CR-03B · Normative / conceptual

**EN:** Having no secure way to meet basic needs makes a person less free, even when no law blocks their choices.

**VI:** Không có cách ổn định để đáp ứng các nhu cầu cơ bản khiến một người kém tự do hơn, ngay cả khi không có luật nào ngăn cản những lựa chọn của họ.

**Distinction:** Material conditions as part of freedom. Agreement does not identify the institution that should secure those conditions, or imply support for any particular benefit program.

### CR-03C · Normative / conceptual

**EN:** If workers have no effective way to appeal dismissal, their employer's power to dismiss them reduces their freedom even when that power is never used.

**VI:** Nếu người lao động không có cách khiếu nại hữu hiệu đối với quyết định sa thải, quyền sa thải của người sử dụng lao động làm giảm tự do của họ ngay cả khi quyền đó không bao giờ được sử dụng.

**Distinction:** A standing capacity to exercise power versus actual interference. Agreement does not establish opposition to all dismissals, management authority, or private employment.

**Contrast and probe:** Ask: “Could the job choice be free but unfair? Could a person be materially secure but still depend on someone's goodwill?” For CR-03C, ask what makes an appeal effective, and whether adding independent, enforceable review would change the answer.

**Main risks:** Respondents may silently assume savings, family support, or an abusive employer. CR-03C explicitly removes effective appeal, but still needs a distinction between a polite explanation and a remedy that can constrain the employer. Vietnamese *khiếu nại hữu hiệu* needs a plain-language paraphrase test. If answers depend mainly on an unstated fact, move the distinction into a specified scenario. Do not calculate a freedom continuum from the three responses.

## CR-04 — Desert, need, and particular obligations

**Question:** What grounds a claim on resources or assistance? Relevant debates include liberal accounts of fair opportunity, libertarian entitlement, socialist and social-democratic equality, communitarian obligation, and feminist care ethics. Care ethics is not equivalent to making families solely responsible for care.

### CR-04A · Normative

**EN:** When the same legal rules apply to everyone, people deserve the income they earn even if they start with very different family resources.

**VI:** Khi mọi người đều chịu cùng những quy tắc pháp luật, họ xứng đáng được hưởng khoản thu nhập mình kiếm được, ngay cả khi xuất phát điểm về nguồn lực gia đình rất khác nhau.

**Distinction:** Equal formal rules as a sufficient basis for deserved earnings despite unequal starting resources. Agreement does not establish opposition to taxes, support for every existing law, or a belief that effort alone causes income.

### CR-04B · Normative

**EN:** People with the same needs should receive the same level of public support, even when their past contributions differ.

**VI:** Những người có cùng mức nhu cầu nên nhận được cùng mức hỗ trợ công, ngay cả khi những đóng góp trước đây của họ khác nhau.

**Distinction:** Need relative to contribution in allocating support. Agreement does not establish that all people have identical needs or that every resource should be allocated by need.

### CR-04C · Normative

**EN:** A person has stronger duties to people who depend on their care than to equally needy strangers.

**VI:** Một người có nghĩa vụ lớn hơn đối với những người phụ thuộc vào sự chăm sóc của mình so với những người xa lạ có mức nhu cầu tương đương.

**Distinction:** Relationship-based obligations relative to impartial need. Agreement does not imply that caring duties are unlimited, naturally assigned to women, or exclusively private.

**Contrast and probe:** Ask whether answers change when speaking about a private person, an elected official, or a public institution. Ask what “contribution” includes: paid work, unpaid care, taxes, or another activity. Distinguish *deserving an income* from *being legally entitled to keep it*.

**Main risks:** CR-04A bridges a claim about rules with a moral conclusion; respondents may reject the fairness of the imagined rules rather than the sufficiency claim. CR-04B can mean equal money or equal effective support. CR-04C concerns an individual's obligations and should not be generalized to a state's duties. Vietnamese *xứng đáng*, *đóng góp*, and *nghĩa vụ* require particular attention.

## CR-05 — Who should govern an enterprise?

**Question:** What gives someone a claim to make binding economic decisions: ownership, work, or dependence on the service? Relevant debates include property-based liberal and libertarian arguments, social-democratic co-determination, democratic-socialist and Marxist accounts of social control, anarchist self-management, and stakeholder approaches. Public ownership does not by itself establish worker or user control.

### CR-05A · Institutional / normative

**EN:** In an enterprise owned by people who do not work there, those owners should have final authority over its main decisions, even when workers oppose them.

**VI:** Trong một doanh nghiệp do những người không làm việc tại đó sở hữu, các chủ sở hữu này nên có quyền quyết định cuối cùng về những vấn đề chủ chốt, ngay cả khi người lao động phản đối.

**Distinction:** Non-working ownership as a basis of final authority. The stated ownership arrangement keeps owners and workers distinct in this case without assuming that all enterprises are organized this way. Agreement does not establish support for unlimited authority, exemption from law, or the claim that investors alone bear risks.

### CR-05B · Institutional / normative

**EN:** Workers should be able to elect a majority of the body that sets their enterprise's main policies.

**VI:** Người lao động nên có quyền bầu ra đa số thành viên của cơ quan quyết định các chính sách chủ chốt của doanh nghiệp nơi họ làm việc.

**Distinction:** Worker majority control, rather than consultation or a minority of seats. Agreement does not establish employee ownership, opposition to managers with specialist skills, or a preferred economy-wide allocation system.

### CR-05C · Institutional / normative

**EN:** An enterprise providing an essential service should reserve voting seats on its governing body for people who use that service.

**VI:** Một doanh nghiệp cung cấp dịch vụ thiết yếu nên dành ghế có quyền biểu quyết trong cơ quan quản trị cho những người sử dụng dịch vụ đó.

**Distinction:** User representation beyond owner or producer authority. Agreement does not establish a user majority, state ownership, or direct votes on every operational choice.

**Contrast and probe:** Ask which decisions the respondent imagined: investment, wages, service eligibility, prices, or closure. Ask how a worker-owned enterprise should handle a conflict between its members and service users. A respondent can endorse worker control for ordinary enterprises and additional user representation for an essential service.

**Main risks:** “Enterprise,” “governing body,” and “essential service” need concrete paraphrases; different legal systems make different models familiar. Expected competence or investment flight may drive answers even when the stem asks about rightful authority. Record those causal expectations separately. A cooperative must not automatically be treated as a midpoint between public and private provision.

## CR-06 — Statistical evidence, experience, and dispersed knowledge

**Question:** Which grounds of knowledge deserve weight, and what knowledge can institutions gather? Relevant debates include empirical and technocratic approaches, feminist social epistemology, anti-colonial critiques of recognized expertise, market knowledge arguments, and decentralized anarchist coordination. None of these labels settles whether a particular source is reliable.

### CR-06A · Epistemic

**EN:** When a statistical study and residents' accounts conflict, the study is usually the more reliable starting point.

**VI:** Khi một nghiên cứu thống kê mâu thuẫn với lời kể của người dân, nghiên cứu đó thường là điểm xuất phát đáng tin cậy hơn.

**Distinction:** A default evidential priority when sources conflict. Agreement does not establish blind trust in statistics; disagreement may mean insufficient information about either source rather than rejection of research.

### CR-06B · Epistemic

**EN:** Personal experience of exclusion is a reason to give someone's explanation of that exclusion extra weight.

**VI:** Việc một người từng trực tiếp trải qua sự loại trừ là một lý do để coi lời giải thích của họ về sự loại trừ đó có sức nặng hơn.

**Distinction:** Situated experience as an epistemic reason. Agreement does not establish infallibility, unanimity within a group, or exclusive authority to speak about it.

### CR-06C · Explanatory / epistemic

**EN:** Information held by buyers and sellers is often too specific to their circumstances for a central planner to collect it in time.

**VI:** Thông tin mà người mua và người bán nắm giữ thường gắn quá chặt với hoàn cảnh riêng của họ để một cơ quan kế hoạch tập trung có thể thu thập kịp thời.

**Distinction:** Context-specific and time-sensitive knowledge limits. Agreement does not establish that prices transmit all relevant knowledge, that markets are fair, or that participatory planning is impossible.

**Contrast and probe:** Ask what makes a source reliable and what new information would reverse its priority. For CR-06A, ask whether the sources are actually describing the same outcome. For CR-06C, distinguish “impossible to know everything” from “unable to gather enough information for this decision.”

**Main risks:** These are default judgments under underspecification, so “it depends on study quality” is informative rather than a bad answer. CR-06A could mostly measure trust in a familiar authority, CR-06B recognition or courtesy, and CR-06C views about an imagined planning technology. If participants cannot supply a stable intended comparison, use a case with specified evidence instead. Do not collapse the three into expertise versus experience.

## CR-07 — Inheritance, injustice, and the pace of change

**Question:** What justifies preserving an institution, and what justifies changing it before outcomes are certain? Relevant debates include conservative presumptions for continuity, reformist and revolutionary socialist strategies, liberal institutionalism, republican contestation, and pragmatic experimentation. Gradualism need not express attachment to an inherited social order.

### CR-07A · Institutional / normative

**EN:** An inherited institution deserves to continue unless its critics show that a replacement would work better.

**VI:** Một thiết chế được kế thừa từ trước xứng đáng được duy trì, trừ khi những người phê phán chứng minh rằng một thiết chế thay thế sẽ hoạt động tốt hơn.

**Distinction:** A presumption and burden of proof favoring the existing institution. Agreement does not establish approval of every tradition or immunity from criticism.

### CR-07B · Institutional / normative

**EN:** If a political institution denies some adults an equal voice, replacing it may be justified before an alternative has been fully tested.

**VI:** Nếu một thiết chế chính trị không cho một số người trưởng thành có tiếng nói bình đẳng, việc thay thế nó có thể là chính đáng trước khi một phương án khác được kiểm chứng đầy đủ.

**Distinction:** Political exclusion as a reason to act despite uncertainty about a replacement. Agreement does not establish support for violence, abrupt abolition of all services, or disregard of foreseeable consequences.

### CR-07C · Institutional

**EN:** Reform should normally proceed through several reversible steps, even when a complete redesign could deliver its benefits sooner.

**VI:** Cải cách thông thường nên được tiến hành qua nhiều bước có thể đảo ngược, ngay cả khi việc thiết kế lại toàn bộ có thể đem lại lợi ích sớm hơn.

**Distinction:** Reversibility as a reason for incremental change. Agreement does not establish that the current institution deserves moral deference or that all major changes can be reversed.

**Contrast and probe:** Ask what “work better” includes: efficiency, justice, continuity, participation, or something else. Then ask whether the case for gradual change rests on attachment to the institution or uncertainty about alternatives. CR-07A/B can coexist because unjust exclusion may itself count as evidence that a replacement would be better.

**Main risks:** The three stems embed different burdens and probabilities. “May be justified” in CR-07B is deliberately weaker than “usually should”; test whether this becomes easy assent without a useful distinction. CR-07C depends on the assumed damage of waiting, not merely risk aversion. Never score gradualism as conservatism by definition.

## CR-08 — Membership, duties, and collective self-rule

**Question:** Who belongs to a political community, and what does membership change? Relevant debates include civic and cultural nationalism, liberal citizenship, cosmopolitan duties, communitarian obligations, and anti-colonial self-determination. Citizenship, residence, cultural belonging, and a people's claim to rule itself are different subjects.

### CR-08A · Normative

**EN:** A government owes stronger duties to its citizens than to non-citizens with equally serious needs.

**VI:** Một chính phủ có nghĩa vụ lớn hơn đối với công dân của mình so với những người không phải công dân nhưng có nhu cầu nghiêm trọng tương đương.

**Distinction:** Special duties of political membership relative to equal need. Agreement does not establish hostility toward foreigners, opposition to immigration, or rejection of universal minimum duties.

### CR-08B · Normative / institutional

**EN:** People who settle permanently in a country should be able to become full political members without adopting the majority's way of life.

**VI:** Những người định cư lâu dài ở một quốc gia nên có thể trở thành thành viên chính trị đầy đủ mà không phải tiếp nhận lối sống của đa số.

**Distinction:** Political inclusion relative to cultural assimilation. Agreement does not establish that membership has no conditions, that laws need not be followed, or that language requirements are always unacceptable.

### CR-08C · Normative

**EN:** A political community ruled by outsiders has a claim to collective self-rule even when equal individual rights are already protected.

**VI:** Một cộng đồng chính trị do người bên ngoài cai quản có quyền đòi hỏi tự quản tập thể ngay cả khi các quyền cá nhân bình đẳng đã được bảo vệ.

**Distinction:** Collective self-rule as a claim not exhausted by individual rights. Agreement does not establish which groups constitute a people, endorsement of every secession claim, or acceptance of minority exclusion within the new unit.

**Contrast and probe:** Ask: “What creates membership here—citizenship, residence, shared culture, consent, or something else?” For CR-08C, ask who counts as an outsider and who can contest a claim to speak for the community. Do not supply a real-country example before the respondent gives their own interpretation.

**Main risks:** “Full political members” can mean citizenship, voting rights, or social recognition. The normative force of self-rule depends partly on a contested account of the community; that ontology should be recorded rather than presumed. This cluster does not measure a single nationalism–globalism axis, and its answers cannot identify any respondent's nationality.

## CR-09 — Religion and public justification

**Question:** What can justify binding law, and what relationship should public institutions have to religion? Relevant debates include religious political traditions, liberal public reason, secularism, accommodation, and establishment. Religious approaches contain substantial disagreements about coercion, pluralism, interpretation, and the state's authority; this cluster is not a scale of personal religiosity.

### CR-09A · Normative / justificatory

**EN:** A law can be justified by religious teaching even when citizens who reject that teaching have no other reason to accept the law.

**VI:** Một luật có thể được biện minh bằng giáo lý tôn giáo ngay cả khi những công dân không chấp nhận giáo lý đó không có lý do nào khác để chấp nhận luật ấy.

**Distinction:** Religious teaching as sufficient public justification. Agreement does not establish support for every religious interpretation, preference for a particular religion, or a claim that religious authority should directly govern.

### CR-09B · Normative / justificatory

**EN:** Officials should defend binding laws with reasons that people of different religions, or none, can assess without accepting one faith.

**VI:** Quan chức nên biện minh cho các luật có tính bắt buộc bằng những lý do mà người theo các tôn giáo khác nhau, hoặc không theo tôn giáo nào, có thể xem xét mà không phải chấp nhận một đức tin cụ thể.

**Distinction:** Public assessability of reasons across faith commitments. Agreement does not require actual consensus, prohibit religious motivation, or exclude religious people from public debate.

### CR-09C · Institutional / normative

**EN:** Assume everyone's freedom of religious or non-religious belief is equally protected. Public funds should support worship activities of the country's majority religion.

**VI:** Giả sử tự do có niềm tin tôn giáo hoặc phi tôn giáo của mọi người đều được bảo vệ bình đẳng. Ngân sách công nên hỗ trợ các hoạt động thờ phụng của tôn giáo mà đa số người dân trong nước theo.

**Distinction:** Public funding for majority-religion worship, with equal freedom of belief fixed as a condition rather than a second proposition to endorse. Agreement does not establish exclusive funding for that religion, an official religious test for office, or the legal inferiority of other believers.

**Contrast and probe:** Ask whether the judgment concerns a law's origin, the reasons officials must offer, or the law's effects. For CR-09C, ask whether the respondent distinguished funding worship from preserving historic buildings or funding non-religious services run by religious groups. Ask whether the same funding judgment is accepted when another religion is the majority.

**Main risks:** CR-09A and CR-09B can both be endorsed if “justified” means morally justified in one but publicly authorized in the other. Test whether respondents accept the stipulated equal liberty in CR-09C or instead answer about their expectation that funding would undermine it; preserve that distinction. The item does not specify a comparative allocation across religions, so it cannot establish majority preference over equal funding. Country-specific expectations of religion and the state are not a measure of theological belief.

## CR-10 — Inherited status, repair, and representation

**Question:** What follows from exclusion transmitted across generations? Relevant debates include anti-caste political thought, anti-racist and Black political thought, formal-equality liberalism, substantive-equality arguments, feminism, and accounts of historical repair. These are connected debates, **not interchangeable histories**. Three generic items cannot represent anti-caste thought or any affected community comprehensively.

### CR-10A · Institutional / normative

**EN:** Imagine a society where birth assigns people to ranked groups that restrict marriage and job opportunities. Public assistance to remedy this discrimination should be based on individual need rather than inherited group membership.

**VI:** Hãy hình dung một xã hội nơi con người được xếp vào các nhóm có thứ bậc ngay từ khi sinh ra, với những hạn chế về kết hôn và việc làm theo nhóm. Hỗ trợ công nhằm khắc phục sự phân biệt đối xử này nên dựa trên nhu cầu của từng cá nhân thay vì việc một người thuộc nhóm nào từ khi sinh ra.

**Distinction:** Individual need versus inherited group membership as the eligibility basis for remedial assistance in a specified system of social closure. Agreement does not establish opposition to recording discrimination, denial of its history, or a general prohibition on every use of group identity.

### CR-10B · Normative

**EN:** People today owe repair for advantages they still receive from earlier exclusion, even if they did not cause it.

**VI:** Con người ngày nay có nghĩa vụ góp phần khắc phục vì những lợi thế họ vẫn được hưởng từ sự loại trừ trong quá khứ, ngay cả khi chính họ không gây ra sự loại trừ đó.

**Distinction:** Continuing benefit as a ground of present responsibility, separate from personal causation or guilt. Agreement does not specify compensation, punishment, a policy instrument, or the extent of a duty.

### CR-10C · Institutional / normative

**EN:** People from groups kept out of decision-making should have guaranteed seats in public bodies, even when ordinary elections are open to everyone.

**VI:** Những người thuộc các nhóm bị gạt khỏi quá trình ra quyết định nên được bảo đảm ghế trong các cơ quan công quyền, ngay cả khi mọi người đều được tham gia các cuộc bầu cử thông thường.

**Distinction:** Guaranteed group representation beyond formally open elections. Agreement does not establish who selects representatives, how groups are defined, or whether reserved seats should be permanent.

**Contrast and probe:** Ask respondents to paraphrase the birth-assigned hierarchy in CR-10A and explain whether an individual's “need” includes harms from group discrimination. Ask whether repair is owed because of wrongdoing, continuing benefit, ongoing disadvantage, or equal citizenship. For representation, probe whether participants imagine an elected representative, an appointment, or a veto.

**Main risks:** CR-10A defines a fictional inherited hierarchy rather than equating caste, class, and race. It still abstracts from distinct histories and does not represent anti-caste thought adequately on its own. Respondents may reasonably combine need with group membership; record that position rather than forcing it into a false binary. “Earlier exclusion” in CR-10B may be too abstract without an example. Group labels can also freeze disputed identities. Dedicated anti-caste, Black, and other affected-community scholarship and review are needed before any coverage claim.

## CR-11 — Ecological standing and limits

**Question:** Do ecological obligations rest on present human benefit, future human needs, non-human standing, or limits to permissible substitution? Relevant debates include ecological and environmental political thought, liberal and socialist environmental justice, intergenerational ethics, and particular Indigenous traditions where supported by their own scholarship. Do not label an item “Indigenous” because it values nature.

### CR-11A · Normative

**EN:** Protecting a living ecosystem can be a political duty even when doing so brings no benefit to people.

**VI:** Bảo vệ một hệ sinh thái đang tồn tại có thể là một nghĩa vụ chính trị ngay cả khi việc đó không đem lại lợi ích nào cho con người.

**Distinction:** Non-instrumental ecological standing. Agreement does not establish equal moral standing for every organism or a rule for every conflict with human needs.

### CR-11B · Normative

**EN:** People living today may have a duty to accept a lower standard of living to protect the basic needs of future generations.

**VI:** Những người đang sống có thể có nghĩa vụ chấp nhận mức sống thấp hơn để bảo vệ các nhu cầu cơ bản của những thế hệ tương lai.

**Distinction:** Obligations across generations involving a present sacrifice. Agreement does not establish that all income groups owe the same sacrifice or that every present luxury and basic need has equal weight.

### CR-11C · Normative / institutional

**EN:** There should be ecological limits that even a project with large net public benefits is not allowed to cross.

**VI:** Cần có những giới hạn sinh thái mà ngay cả một dự án đem lại lợi ích ròng lớn cho công chúng cũng không được phép vượt qua.

**Distinction:** Constraints on trading ecological damage for aggregate benefit. Agreement does not identify the limits, deny all technology-based solutions, or establish that every ecological loss is irreplaceable.

**Contrast and probe:** Ask whether “no benefit” includes spiritual, cultural, aesthetic, and future benefits, and whether the hypothetical still makes sense. Ask who should bear the reduction in CR-11B. For CR-11C, ask for a limit and a case in which compensation would be acceptable.

**Main risks:** CR-11A and CR-11B use possibility rather than a general priority, which can yield easy agreement; their initial purpose is to locate a reason that needs a concrete follow-up. “Net public benefits” may be read as money alone or as an aggregate already including ecological value. If the latter makes CR-11C circular, replace the term in a scenario with specified benefits and ecological consequences. Do not infer positions on economic growth from these three responses alone.

## CR-12 — Consent, performance, and compulsory coordination

**Question:** What makes binding authority legitimate, and must common problems be coordinated by a state? Relevant debates include democratic and republican authorization, performance-based accounts of legitimacy, anarchist voluntary association, conservative authority, and libertarian limits on coercion. These candidates ask about grounds of authority rather than using an “authoritarian personality” label.

### CR-12A · Institutional / normative

**EN:** Even competent leaders should have only powers that those subject to them can regularly withdraw.

**VI:** Ngay cả những người lãnh đạo có năng lực cũng chỉ nên có những quyền hạn mà những người chịu sự chi phối của họ có thể định kỳ thu hồi.

**Distinction:** Revocable authorization relative to competence. Agreement does not establish a preference for direct democracy, a right to veto every decision, or agreement on who counts among those subject to a power.

### CR-12B · Normative

**EN:** A government that reliably provides security and basic services can have a right to rule without competitive elections.

**VI:** Một chính phủ bảo đảm an ninh và các dịch vụ cơ bản một cách đáng tin cậy có thể có quyền chính đáng để cai trị mà không cần bầu cử có cạnh tranh.

**Distinction:** Performance as a possible basis of legitimacy without electoral competition. Agreement does not establish support for arbitrary repression, hereditary office, or any particular real government.

### CR-12C · Institutional / normative

**EN:** Cooperation on shared problems should normally be organized through voluntary associations rather than a state with the power to compel participation.

**VI:** Việc hợp tác để giải quyết các vấn đề chung thông thường nên được tổ chức thông qua các hiệp hội tự nguyện hơn là thông qua một nhà nước có quyền buộc người dân tham gia.

**Distinction:** A presumption for voluntary association relative to compulsory public coordination. Agreement does not specify whether the associations use markets, common ownership, mutual aid, or another arrangement.

**Contrast and probe:** Ask what would entitle someone to resist an otherwise effective government. For CR-12A, ask whether removing a leader and withdrawing a particular power are different. For CR-12C, ask how the respondent would address a person who benefits without contributing or a problem that crosses association boundaries.

**Main risks:** “Right to rule” must be distinguished from legal power and ability to keep order; the Vietnamese draft uses *quyền chính đáng* to make legitimacy explicit. CR-12A may be read as elections alone, although other revocation procedures are possible. CR-12C bridges a moral preference for voluntary coordination with empirical expectations about its feasibility. No single response establishes a person's regime preference across all circumstances.

## Cross-cluster review decisions

The bank deliberately permits combinations that a simple compass obscures: accepting material inequality while demanding worker voice; supporting religious reasons while requiring public assessability; accepting electoral democracy while doubting that votes offset economic power; supporting collective self-rule while rejecting cultural assimilation. These are examples of logically possible readings, not predictions about who will answer that way.

Priority questions for review are:

1. **Does the item add a distinction?** Compare the explanations given, not just whether mean agreement differs. Drop an item whose only extra information is that a participant disliked its vocabulary.
2. **Is it one interpretable proposition?** A useful stipulated condition is not automatically a double-barrel, but reviewers must check whether people can accept one part and reject an independently asserted second claim. Split or move mixed claims into situations where the condition can be explicit.
3. **Does agreement reflect the intended reason?** CR-01/02 concern explanations, CR-03 meanings of freedom, and CR-04 moral grounds. It would be an error to treat every answer as a normative policy preference.
4. **Is a general statement the right format?** CR-03C, CR-06A, CR-07C, CR-09C, and CR-11C are especially likely to need specified contexts. Their presence here is an interview hypothesis, not a claim that abstraction always improves measurement.
5. **Can informed critics recognize the dispute?** Seek objections from multiple perspectives within a debate. Do not let a single self-identified representative certify a tradition or use equal numbers of named traditions as proof of fair coverage.
6. **Do both languages preserve scope and strength?** Check *can*, *usually*, *should*, *only*, *even if*, membership terms, and the distinction between a rightful claim and an existing legal power. A fluent translation can still alter the construct.

Remaining gaps include nationalism's competing grounds; religious traditions in their own concepts rather than only their relation to a liberal state; specific anti-caste, Black, Indigenous and anti-colonial arguments; gender and family authority; disability and care institutions; policing and punishment; military power; property in land and data; migration; and international domination. Their inclusion requires further source-led development. This bank adds entry points to some of those disputes without claiming comprehensive representation of world political thought.
