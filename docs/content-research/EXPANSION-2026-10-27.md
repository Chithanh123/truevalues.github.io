## Questions and competing explanations

| Cluster | Question for research and review | Distinction to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CR-13 | Who controls decisions about disability support, and what makes access usable? | Formal access, individual choice and collective service design are not the same. |
| CR-14 | How should household work, bargaining and obligations be understood? | Equal standing does not prescribe identical tasks; unpaid work and voluntary commitments may overlap. |
| CR-15 | What powers and safeguards are appropriate when decisions must be made quickly? | Capacity to protect, legal authorization, speed and accountability can pull in different directions. |
| CR-16 | Who should govern information that affects individuals and groups? | Individual consent, collective consequences, useful processing and contestability are distinct. |
| CR-17 | Who should govern shared resources and how should participants be represented? | Group authority, individual voice and exit are distinct; a fictional commons is not a substitute for Indigenous self-determination. |
| CR-18 | How can people who work under insecure arrangements act together? | Individual flexibility, collective bargaining and the cost of organizing can coexist. |
| CR-19 | How are production, returns, investment and unpaid work related? | A claim about who creates income differs from a claim about who is entitled to decide its use. |
| CR-20 | How do material arrangements, law, institutions and culture affect one another? | Economic conditions can constrain institutions; ideas and institutions can also shape economic life. These are competing explanations, not a settled one-way model. |
| CR-21 | What kinds of entities and relationships make up social reality? | Individual agency, collective entities, material conditions and emergent relations need not be mutually exclusive. |
| CR-22 | How can political knowledge be established and challenged? | Expertise, lived experience, evidence quality and disagreement are not mutually exclusive sources of knowledge. |
| CR-23 | How should competing social explanations be investigated? | Comparisons, historical interpretation, lived meaning and participatory inquiry can answer different questions and be combined. |
| CR-24 | How do security, shared expectations, institutions and sovereignty influence relations between countries? | Conflicting interests, shared rules, material capabilities and ideas are distinct possible explanations; they can operate together. |

The base/superstructure distinction is useful as a research question: whether and how production and economic relations condition political, legal and cultural arrangements. A response to one statement cannot establish that a participant accepts Marx's account, a liberal account, or any other complete theory. Direction of influence, time scale, institutional autonomy and historical context must be specified before comparing explanations. The new bank also asks about alternatives and reciprocal influence, so agreement with a single economic premise does not define the rest of a person's outlook.

**Ontology** concerns what exists and how social entities or relationships are constituted. **Epistemology** concerns how people can know and justify claims about them. **Methodology** concerns how questions are investigated and evidence compared. A moral or institutional preference is a separate kind of claim. The candidates use ordinary language and record these types individually; they do not assign a philosophical identity or calculate a total for each category.

## What the source checks establish

On 27 October, selected passages from six sources were inspected: official W3C repository snapshots on privacy and accessibility, and public-domain mirrors of Mill, Hobbes, Kropotkin and Locke. Each source record identifies the exact snapshot, passage, short excerpt, and its limits. For example, the W3C privacy text distinguishes group effects from individual consent; it does not establish which institution should govern a public database. Locke's discussion of prerogative shows an argument for executive discretion within a historical liberal text; it does not settle the design of a contemporary emergency power.

Direct requests for the CRPD, ICCPR, UN declaration and ILO convention were blocked by the network proxy. Marx, Weber, Gramsci and several other proposed readings have not been retrieved and verified in this increment. Their rows are a reading queue, not verified attribution. The inspected public-domain mirrors have not been compared with critical editions. The source list is selective and does not adequately represent the breadth of Indigenous, Black, Asian, disability or other political traditions. A source can illuminate an author's question without validating the wording of a proposed assessment item.

## Review before adoption

1. Check the original readings and rival interpretations, particularly the economic and social-theory sources. Invite reviewers familiar with relevant traditions and lived experience rather than assigning one reviewer to represent a whole tradition.
2. Review the English and Vietnamese drafts independently, including political vocabulary, the meaning of consent and majority approval, and whether the same numerical constraints are understood. Vietnamese wording remains an author draft.
3. Test the situations' actual decision powers, funding, options and tradeoffs in the fictional institutions. Eight country wrappers require separate feasibility work; real-country inspirations stay hidden in the participant flow until the final debrief.
4. Conduct comprehension interviews with small packets. Separate understanding from agreement, keep uncertainty and skips available, and record the reason a person chose an option. The proposed controlled variations are editorial probes; a changed answer does not by itself establish a causal effect.
5. Select a manageable form only after review. The extra questions expand the researcher database, not the existing 49-item route. They have no option scores and should not be used as a political-identity classifier or a validated ontology, epistemology or methodology scale.
