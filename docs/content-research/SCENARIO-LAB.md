# Scenario laboratory: richer decisions before implementation

Prepared 23 October 2026; revised 25 October 2026. **Content proposals for review, not implemented questions, validated dilemmas, or findings about participants.** Instrument `0.4.0` and its existing 49-item route remain unchanged. All numbers, institutions, permissions, capacities, and estimates below are fictional stipulations. None describes the present law or population of a real country. The English–Vietnamese examples are author drafts awaiting independent review.

The purpose is to distinguish reasons that the current market/public, domestic/international, order/liberty, and continuity/change summaries often combine. A choice of a public provider, for example, can express a preference for equality, predictable delivery, employee control, or an effective appeal. Increasing the number of stories without separating those interpretations would add burden without necessarily adding information.

## Common content contract

Each family has a concrete role, an authorized decision, a budget or capacity limit, an opportunity cost for every package, and a counter-explanation for an apparently ideological choice. The options are **not assigned ideological scores or arranged as left/middle/right**. A combination is not automatically moderate; an attractive-sounding safeguard is not automatically more liberal or democratic in every political account.

Use the following response sequence for paper review. The exact interface and number of follow-up questions remain undecided:

1. Choose the action you would take **within the stated role**; alternatively, choose “uncertain,” “none of these represents my decision,” or “skip.” Do not force a choice that a participant finds infeasible or conceptually false.
2. Ask for the most important reason in the participant's own words before showing researcher-proposed reason labels in an interview. Allow an additional reason, uncertainty, and a reason not listed. Reasons are unscored and need not match an author's expected option/reason combination.
3. For selected cases, ask which package they personally prefer under the **same resources, legal permissions, and facts**, setting aside their assigned responsibility. Do not assume that a difference reveals hypocrisy or that a personal preference is more authentic.
4. In interviews, ask which stated fact would most change the decision. Use the prepared variant on separate paper only when the interview design calls for it; do not silently change facts mid-answer.

Give every package equivalent visual prominence. Counterbalance option position only in a later, documented form design; option-order effects are not repaired by renaming them. Present the necessary institutional facts before the decision, but keep political-tradition labels and real-country correspondences out of the participant blocks. Reveal inspirations in the final debrief; only then ask whether readers had already suspected an inspiration and whether that affected their reading. This is retrospective self-report after disclosure, not an independent measure of recognition. Fictional institutions may still allow inference; this is delayed disclosure, not secure blinding.

## CR-S01 — Who can change the housing rules?

**Research target.** Distinguish the ability to leave a relationship, collective control over its rules, and an individual right to contest power. Keep municipal ownership fixed so that collective voice cannot be read simply as public-ownership preference. This concerns ordinary lawful rules, not permission to remove basic tenant rights.

### Participant draft: English

**Your surroundings.** You live in a municipal housing estate with 120 households in a large city in your assigned fictional country. The estate manager can change rules about courtyard opening hours, visitors, and use of the meeting room after giving 30 days' notice. In the past year the manager has used that power twice. The existing rules are lawful. Residents disagree about whether those rules are reasonable.

**Your role.** You are an ordinary resident answering a consultation. You can recommend a package; you cannot spend the budget, appoint officials, or impose a rule. The municipal council will decide.

**What is fixed.** The council has set aside 90,000 credits for one two-year package. Municipal ownership, rent levels, repair duties, protection from eviction, and existing legal rights remain the same under every package. All packages have approved funding and can operate for the full two years. Ten suitable homes at the same rent are available elsewhere for households that want to move; there is no promise of further vacancies.

**Which package would you recommend?**

| Option | What the package does | What it gives up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Make leaving affordable | Fund moving expenses of 9,000 credits for each of ten households that wish to leave. If more than ten apply, allocate the grants by lottery. The manager keeps the present rule-making power. | Households that stay gain no additional control over those rules; only ten households receive moving support. |
| B — Give residents a collective decision | Fund accessible elections, meeting support, and independent advice for a resident board. A new rule about the three listed subjects needs the board's approval. Urgent safety repairs remain outside its veto. | Seeking agreement can delay ordinary rule changes by up to 60 days, and an individual resident can still lose a board vote. |
| C — Give each resident a way to challenge a rule | Fund an independent reviewer and free help with submissions. Any resident may challenge a rule as unreasonable. The reviewer can reverse it, with a decision due within 15 working days. Funding covers all eligible complaints. | A rule can operate while a case is heard; residents gain no collective vote over the manager's future proposals. |

You may also answer “uncertain,” “none of these represents my decision,” or “skip.” All three packages remain lawful in this fictional setting.

### Bản nháp dành cho người tham gia: Tiếng Việt

**Bối cảnh của bạn.** Bạn sống tại một khu nhà ở thuộc sở hữu của thành phố, gồm 120 hộ, trong một đô thị lớn của quốc gia giả định được giao. Sau khi thông báo trước 30 ngày, người quản lý khu nhà có thể thay đổi quy định về giờ mở sân chung, khách đến thăm và việc sử dụng phòng sinh hoạt chung. Trong năm qua, người quản lý đã sử dụng quyền này hai lần. Các quy định hiện hành đều hợp pháp. Cư dân có ý kiến khác nhau về việc các quy định đó có hợp lý hay không.

**Vai trò của bạn.** Bạn là một cư dân bình thường, đang góp ý trong đợt tham vấn. Bạn có thể đề xuất một phương án; bạn không có quyền chi ngân sách, bổ nhiệm người phụ trách hoặc ban hành quy định. Hội đồng thành phố sẽ quyết định.

**Những điều được giữ nguyên.** Hội đồng đã dành 90.000 đơn vị tiền giả định cho một phương án kéo dài hai năm. Trong cả ba phương án, quyền sở hữu của thành phố, mức thuê, nghĩa vụ sửa chữa, quy định bảo vệ người thuê khỏi việc bị buộc rời nhà và các quyền pháp lý hiện có đều không thay đổi. Mọi phương án đã được duyệt kinh phí và có thể hoạt động đủ hai năm. Ở nơi khác có mười căn nhà phù hợp với mức thuê bằng hiện tại, dành cho những hộ muốn chuyển đi; không có cam kết sẽ có thêm nhà trống.

**Bạn sẽ đề xuất phương án nào?**

| Phương án | Nội dung | Điều phải đánh đổi |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Hỗ trợ để có thể chuyển đi | Chi 9.000 đơn vị tiền hỗ trợ chuyển nhà cho mỗi hộ trong số mười hộ muốn rời khu nhà. Nếu có hơn mười hộ đăng ký, các suất hỗ trợ được phân bổ bằng bốc thăm. Người quản lý giữ quyền đặt quy định như hiện nay. | Các hộ ở lại không có thêm quyền kiểm soát những quy định này; chỉ mười hộ được hỗ trợ chuyển nhà. |
| B — Để cư dân cùng quyết định | Chi cho việc tổ chức bầu cử mà cư dân có thể tham gia, hỗ trợ họp và tư vấn độc lập cho ban đại diện cư dân. Một quy định mới về ba vấn đề đã nêu phải được ban đại diện đồng ý. Ban không có quyền ngăn việc sửa chữa khẩn cấp để bảo đảm an toàn. | Việc tìm sự đồng thuận có thể làm chậm thay đổi quy định thông thường tối đa 60 ngày; một cư dân vẫn có thể thua trong cuộc biểu quyết của ban. |
| C — Cho từng cư dân quyền yêu cầu xem xét quy định | Chi cho một người xem xét độc lập và hỗ trợ miễn phí khi nộp yêu cầu. Bất kỳ cư dân nào cũng có thể đề nghị xem xét một quy định mà họ cho là không hợp lý. Người xem xét có quyền hủy quy định đó và phải quyết định trong 15 ngày làm việc. Kinh phí đủ xử lý mọi yêu cầu đủ điều kiện. | Quy định có thể vẫn được áp dụng trong thời gian xem xét; cư dân không có thêm quyền biểu quyết tập thể đối với các đề xuất sau này của người quản lý. |

Bạn cũng có thể chọn “chưa chắc,” “không phương án nào thể hiện quyết định của tôi,” hoặc “bỏ qua.” Trong bối cảnh giả định này, cả ba phương án đều hợp pháp.

### Reasons and interpretation

Candidate reason labels for later review: ability to leave an unwanted relationship / `khả năng rời một quan hệ mình không muốn`; shared authority over common rules / `quyền cùng quyết định quy tắc chung`; protection when an individual loses a majority vote / `bảo vệ cá nhân khi họ thuộc phía thiểu số trong biểu quyết`; quickest useful help / `hỗ trợ có ích nhanh nhất`; confidence that the mechanism will work / `tin rằng cơ chế sẽ hoạt động`; other reason / `lý do khác`.

Do not enforce option/reason pairing. A respondent might choose A because ten households have urgent needs, because competing housing providers discipline managers, or because they distrust representation. B might express republican contestation, collective self-government, conservative stewardship of a shared place, or simple expectations of better rules. C might protect minorities, limit all officeholders, or minimize meeting burdens. None uniquely identifies a tradition.

**One-factor variant:** replace “In the past year the manager has used that power twice” with “In the past year the manager has not used that power.” Retain the manager's legal capacity, notice period, housing supply, all option text, costs, and safeguards. This tests sensitivity to exercised interference while the capacity to interfere is constant. Changed answers could also reflect inferred trustworthiness or urgency; they do not establish an individual causal estimate or a definitive non-domination belief. Ask whether the participant imagined unstated misconduct.

**Cognitive probes:** Which decisions can you personally make? Does lawful mean reasonable? Does a resident board necessarily speak for you? What happens to the 110 households without a moving grant? What did “independent” mean to you? In Vietnamese, check whether `người xem xét` sounds like a mediator who lacks binding authority; the reversal power must remain explicit.

## CR-S02 — Who receives the extra respite hours?

**Research target.** Separate attention to the care recipient, attention to the caring relationship, and equal additional provision. All options use the same public care service; no response is scored as public/private preference or as a measure of compassion.

### Participant draft: English

**Your surroundings.** You administer a respite-care service in a rural district of your assigned fictional country. Forty separate households receive the service; in this exercise, each has one adult care recipient and one main unpaid carer. Some carers are relatives and some are friends. Every care recipient already receives the same two hours of weekly respite support. Other funded services meet immediate safety needs under every package below.

**Your role.** You are a non-elected service officer with written authority to select one of the three approved allocation rules for the next twelve weeks. You cannot change the eligibility rules, employ extra workers, or require a relative or friend to provide care.

**What is fixed.** An additional 40 care-worker hours are available each week. Travel time and administration are already funded. Independent assessments identify twenty recipients with the greatest additional personal support needs and twenty unpaid carers with the greatest current exhaustion and loss of rest. Ten households belong to both groups. Assessments of both kinds of need are equally reliable. All carers are currently free to stop without penalty and say they want to continue caring under any of these packages. When the caring arrangements began, the carers freely agreed to them. Every recipient can refuse an offered visit without losing existing support.

**Which allocation rule would you use?**

| Option | Extra weekly hours | What it leaves unmet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Prioritize recipients' additional needs | Give two extra hours to each of the twenty recipients with the greatest additional personal support needs. | Ten households with the most exhausted carers receive no extra hours because their recipients are outside this priority group. |
| B — Prioritize carers' exhaustion | Give two extra hours to each of the twenty households whose unpaid carer has the greatest exhaustion and loss of rest. | Ten recipients with the greatest additional personal support needs receive no extra hours because their carers are outside this priority group. |
| C — Give everyone an equal increase | Give one extra hour to each of the forty recipients. | Nobody receives a two-hour increase, including those with the greatest recipient needs or carer exhaustion. |

All packages allocate exactly forty extra hours. An allocation is an offer of support, not permission to compel a visit. Existing support, safety services, assessment standards, consent, and appeal rights stay the same. You may also answer “uncertain,” “none of these represents my decision,” or “skip.”

### Bản nháp dành cho người tham gia: Tiếng Việt

**Bối cảnh của bạn.** Bạn phụ trách một dịch vụ chăm sóc thay thế tạm thời để người chăm sóc thường xuyên có thời gian nghỉ, tại một huyện nông thôn của quốc gia giả định được giao. Bốn mươi hộ riêng biệt sử dụng dịch vụ; trong tình huống này, mỗi hộ có một người trưởng thành cần được chăm sóc và một người chăm sóc chính không hưởng lương. Một số người chăm sóc là người thân; số khác là bạn bè. Mỗi người được chăm sóc hiện có cùng hai giờ hỗ trợ mỗi tuần. Trong mọi phương án dưới đây, các dịch vụ khác đã được cấp kinh phí đáp ứng nhu cầu an toàn trước mắt.

**Vai trò của bạn.** Bạn là viên chức phụ trách dịch vụ, không giữ chức vụ dân cử, và được giao bằng văn bản quyền chọn một trong ba quy tắc phân bổ đã được phê duyệt cho mười hai tuần tới. Bạn không có quyền thay đổi điều kiện được hưởng dịch vụ, thuê thêm nhân viên hoặc bắt buộc người thân hay bạn bè cung cấp việc chăm sóc.

**Những điều được giữ nguyên.** Mỗi tuần có thêm 40 giờ làm việc của nhân viên chăm sóc. Thời gian đi lại và công việc hành chính đã có kinh phí riêng. Đánh giá độc lập xác định hai mươi người được chăm sóc có nhu cầu hỗ trợ cá nhân bổ sung cao nhất, và hai mươi người chăm sóc không hưởng lương đang kiệt sức, thiếu thời gian nghỉ nhiều nhất. Mười hộ thuộc cả hai nhóm. Các đánh giá về hai loại nhu cầu có độ tin cậy như nhau. Hiện nay, mọi người chăm sóc đều có thể dừng việc chăm sóc mà không bị phạt, và đều nói rằng họ muốn tiếp tục trong cả ba phương án. Khi việc chăm sóc bắt đầu, họ đã tự nguyện đồng ý đảm nhận. Mỗi người được chăm sóc có quyền từ chối một buổi hỗ trợ được đề nghị mà không mất phần hỗ trợ đang có.

**Bạn sẽ dùng quy tắc phân bổ nào?**

| Phương án | Số giờ bổ sung mỗi tuần | Nhu cầu chưa được đáp ứng thêm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Ưu tiên nhu cầu bổ sung của người được chăm sóc | Cấp thêm hai giờ cho mỗi người trong số hai mươi người có nhu cầu hỗ trợ cá nhân bổ sung cao nhất. | Mười hộ có người chăm sóc thuộc nhóm kiệt sức nhất không được thêm giờ, vì người được chăm sóc trong hộ không thuộc nhóm ưu tiên này. |
| B — Ưu tiên tình trạng kiệt sức của người chăm sóc | Cấp thêm hai giờ cho mỗi hộ trong số hai mươi hộ có người chăm sóc không hưởng lương đang kiệt sức, thiếu thời gian nghỉ nhiều nhất. | Mười người có nhu cầu hỗ trợ cá nhân bổ sung cao nhất không được thêm giờ, vì người chăm sóc của họ không thuộc nhóm ưu tiên này. |
| C — Tăng như nhau cho mọi người | Cấp thêm một giờ cho mỗi người trong số bốn mươi người được chăm sóc. | Không ai được tăng hai giờ, kể cả những người có nhu cầu hỗ trợ cao nhất hoặc có người chăm sóc kiệt sức nhất. |

Mọi phương án đều phân bổ đúng 40 giờ bổ sung. Việc phân bổ là đề nghị hỗ trợ, không phải quyền buộc người nhận chấp nhận một buổi hỗ trợ. Phần hỗ trợ hiện có, dịch vụ bảo đảm an toàn, tiêu chuẩn đánh giá, quyền đồng ý hoặc từ chối và quyền yêu cầu xem xét lại đều giữ nguyên. Bạn cũng có thể chọn “chưa chắc,” “không phương án nào thể hiện quyết định của tôi,” hoặc “bỏ qua.”

### Reasons and interpretation

Candidate reason labels: greatest unmet recipient need / `nhu cầu chưa được đáp ứng cao nhất của người được chăm sóc`; sustaining an unpaid caring relationship / `duy trì quan hệ chăm sóc không hưởng lương`; carers' claims to rest as persons in their own right / `quyền được nghỉ của người chăm sóc với tư cách một cá nhân`; equal shares of additional public support / `phần hỗ trợ công bổ sung bằng nhau`; reducing reliance on contested priority rankings / `giảm sự phụ thuộc vào cách xếp ưu tiên còn gây tranh luận`; responsibility for a voluntarily accepted role / `trách nhiệm đối với vai trò đã tự nguyện nhận`; other reason / `lý do khác`.

A could express recipient-centered capability, triage, or a narrow interpretation of the officer's duty. B could express care ethics, distributive concern for carers, or a consequentialist prediction that exhausted carers will stop providing care. C could express equal standing, skepticism about measurement, or an implementable rule. A respondent could favor B while holding a strongly family-centered worldview. A respondent could favor A while supporting extensive public care overall.

**One-factor variant:** replace the sentence about how care began with “When the caring arrangements began, the carers were assigned the role without being asked.” Current willingness to continue under every package, freedom to stop without penalty, recipient needs, exhaustion, capacity, consent to visits, available actions, and the officer's powers remain fixed. The assignment describes the past; it does not create a present legal or family power to compel care. This is a proposed contrast about the history of consent, not a claim that consent history is independent of all real-world consequences. Probe whether the respondent changed the stipulated current willingness or imagined coercion continuing now. If the variant is understood as changing present coercion or family safety, revise it or drop the contrast.

**Cognitive probes:** Who receives the service and who gains rest? Are the two priority groups the same twenty households? Does equal increase mean equal total need is met? Did you assume that carers are all women, family members, or unemployed? Does the Vietnamese wording convey temporary replacement of care, rather than stopping care or paying the carer? Ask participants to reconstruct the 20/20/10 overlap; use a simple card diagram if the numbers otherwise dominate the interview.

## CR-S03 — What should the new evidence tell us?

**Editorial status: hold as a comprehension probe pending redesign.** The mandated primary task and the stated limitations of option B already risk making this a test of matching methods to purposes. Its current role is to investigate that reading, not to provide ideological evidence or an assessment score. Retain it in the content-interview packet for that explicit purpose; redesign the authorized tradeoff before treating it as an assessment candidate.

**Participant draft, English; Vietnamese translation still needed.**

You lead a city residents' association. Members worry about air pollution near a freight route. A previous short survey sampled only daytime traffic and does not settle present concerns. Your association can commission and publish a study; it cannot close roads, compel companies to provide records, or declare a legal health standard.

Members have authorized 30,000 credits and eight weeks. A technical team can support any of three sampling plans with the same calibrated equipment, independent quality checks, open methods, and privacy protection. A sample reading is equally reliable under all plans. You know no final pollution result. Residents have useful observations about location and timing, but their reports have not been treated as proof that a threshold has been exceeded. The member resolution sets **estimating average exposure across the neighborhood** as the primary task; detecting short, localized peaks is a secondary task.

| Option | Commission | Cost or limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Sample the neighborhood systematically | Choose locations and times through a published probability-sampling plan across the neighborhood. | It supports estimation of the neighborhood average but may include too few observations at a rare suspected hotspot. |
| B — Sample the places residents identify | Let a supported, open resident process nominate locations and times; the technical team checks measurement quality at those sites. | It can investigate reported hotspots but cannot, without additional assumptions, represent average neighborhood exposure. Some residents may be less able to participate. |
| C — Divide the observations | Use half the observations for the probability sample and half for resident-nominated sites. | It addresses both questions with fewer observations for each; estimates for each purpose are less precise than dedicating the full study to it. |

**Decision:** Which study would you commission under your member mandate? Preserve uncertain, unrepresented, and skip responses.

**Unscored reasons to investigate:** follow the agreed research purpose; test overlooked experiences; avoid assuming active residents represent everyone; produce evidence useful for a later challenge; preserve public trust in the association; use the strongest method for a specified question. A, B, and C each permit more than one motive. B is not evidence that a respondent rejects science; A is not evidence that they dismiss residents; C is not automatically the scientifically best compromise.

**One-factor variant:** change only the member resolution to “detecting short, localized peaks is the primary task; estimating average exposure across the neighborhood is a secondary task.” Equipment, sample budget, participation costs, personnel, prior survey, and choices remain identical. A change can reflect attention to the evidential task rather than a political worldview. This is a useful check against attributing every methodological preference to ideology. If an option becomes obviously required by the wording, retain the variant as a comprehension probe rather than a discriminating assessment item.

**Interpretation limit and probe:** institutional trust, probability understanding, beliefs about health severity, and anticipated political usefulness can all influence a response. Ask what the selected study can establish and whose experience it might miss. Selecting a study supplies no pollution finding at this point; its later measurements might establish exceedances at sampled locations and times, subject to the method's limits. Avoid framing technical and lived knowledge as mutually exclusive kinds of person.

## CR-S04 — Who has a claim on the community fund?

**Participant draft, English; Vietnamese translation still needed.**

You are the elected leader of a rural district. A public community-benefit fund can pay 100 home-repair grants of 1,000 credits each. Six hundred households meet the same eligibility threshold and have comparably urgent repairs. Three hundred have lived in the district for at least five years; three hundred arrived more recently. A separately verified list identifies 100 households that contributed substantial unpaid work to local services during the past year, including care work. Fifty of these are longer-term households and fifty are newer households. Time, disability, and caring responsibilities mean that not everyone had the same opportunity to contribute.

You can recommend an allocation rule to the council. You cannot create new funding, change an applicant's residency status, or award grants to yourself. An independent office checks eligibility and runs any lottery. Every proposal is authorized by the fictional fund's statute. There are no earlier promises to particular households. The fund's money came from a district levy collected over the past decade and now legally belongs to the public fund, not to individual contributors.

| Option | Recommend | Who gives up a stronger claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — One lottery for all eligible households | Draw all 100 grant recipients from the full pool of 600 eligible households. | Long residence and past contribution receive no additional priority. |
| B — Reserve a share for longer-term residents | Draw 75 recipients from the 300 longer-term households, then draw the remaining 25 from all households not already selected. | Newer households have fewer chances despite comparable repair needs. |
| C — Reserve a share for recent contributors | Draw 75 recipients from the 100 verified contributing households, then draw the remaining 25 from all households not already selected. | Residents unable to contribute have fewer chances despite comparable needs; long residence alone receives no priority. |

**Decision:** Which rule would you recommend? Preserve uncertain, unrepresented, and skip responses. No package makes the fund private or alters general civic rights.

**Unscored reasons to investigate:** equal present membership; continuity of a local community; recognition of contribution; expectations attached to earlier public payments; encouraging future cooperation; avoiding penalties for unequal opportunities to contribute; administrative acceptability. B could express reliance interests rather than ethnic nationalism. C could express reciprocity rather than a belief that low contributors have less human worth. A could express equal civic standing, simple administration, or distrust of both classifications.

**One-factor variant:** replace only the fund-origin sentence with “The fund's money came from national transfers received over the past decade and now legally belongs to the public fund, not to individual contributors.” Preserve the ten-year accumulation period, amount, household counts, public legal status, lack of prior promises, contribution records, eligibility, and lottery design. The contrast explores whether the perceived source of a common resource changes the weight of residence or contribution. It does not isolate attitudes to immigration, nationality, ancestry, or cultural difference. The term “newer household” must not quietly become “foreign household.”

**Cognitive probes:** Can the same household win twice? Does “longer-term” mean five years, ancestry, citizenship, or something you supplied? Is care work included in the contribution list? Who could not volunteer? Is the levy a personal savings account? Before fielding, check that respondents understand the two-stage lotteries without requiring calculation of exact winning probabilities.

## CR-S05 — Jobs and an ecological community

**Participant draft, English; Vietnamese translation still needed.**

You head the national government of your assigned fictional country. Parliament asks you to recommend one authorized development package for a rural coastal district. The district needs paid work. The site includes a wetland with a breeding colony of a fictional bird species. The species has breeding colonies at several protected sites elsewhere. Loss of the local colony would therefore not mean the species becomes extinct.

Each package uses 20 million public credits over ten years; contracts and independent delivery checks make the stated paid positions feasible for that period. The positions have the same weekly hours, pay, employment protections, and start date under every package. All are available to district residents through the same eligibility rules; funded training enables eligible applicants to perform the different tasks. The district has more than 400 eligible residents seeking this work. Other national budgets cannot be transferred to this decision. Every package keeps the same six-month income bridge for residents changing jobs, safety rules, housing access, and protections against forced displacement. All sites have verified public title; no package overrides an unmentioned land claim. Flood protection, drinking-water supply, and human health are held at the same assessed level through separately funded works.

Independent ecological reviewers provide fictional risk estimates for the next ten years. The uncertainty ranges are part of the exercise: they are not measurements from an actual place. No option guarantees the colony against all background natural risks.

| Option | Recommend | Ecological and employment consequences |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Full transport terminal | Build the terminal, supporting 400 paid positions for ten years. | Reviewers estimate a 10–30% chance that this project causes the local breeding colony to disappear. |
| B — Smaller terminal | Reduce the terminal footprint, supporting 200 paid positions for ten years. | Reviewers estimate a 2–8% chance that this project causes the local colony to disappear; fewer jobs are created. |
| C — Wetland restoration | Fund restoration and monitoring, supporting 80 paid positions for ten years, without building the terminal. | The package adds no identified project-caused risk of colony loss, but provides the fewest paid positions. Background ecological risks remain. |

**Your authority:** recommend a package to parliament and specify the approved monitoring contract. You cannot guarantee jobs beyond ten years, waive ecological conditions, or authorize spending without parliamentary approval. Preserve uncertain, unrepresented, and skip responses.

**Unscored reasons to investigate:** employment and household independence; duties to nonhuman life; preventing irreversible loss; caution under uncertain estimates; obligations to future people; a national leader's responsibility to livelihoods; confidence in enforceable conditions. A need not express indifference to nature: it may reflect a high value on local employment and a different view of acceptable risk. C may reflect nonhuman moral standing, anticipated future human benefits, or pessimism about contract enforcement. The last explanation should be recorded even though the case stipulates enforceability; it signals a possible credibility problem in the draft.

**One-factor variant:** replace only the species-range sentences with “This is the species' only remaining breeding colony. Its loss would mean the species becomes extinct.” Preserve the local ecological functions, numbers of jobs, public cost, risk ranges, authority, income bridge, and every option. The changed fact alters the scope and irreversibility of loss. It cannot by itself distinguish intrinsic ecological value from human concern about rarity or future option value.

**Cognitive probes:** What does a 10–30% range mean? Did you read “no additional project risk” as “no risk at all”? Which people bear the lost employment, and did you imagine compensation outside the stated bridge? Would your reason survive if the species were unfamiliar or unattractive? Do not use emotionally unequal images or a charismatic real species to make one package more appealing.

## CR-S06 — Training places after exclusion

**Participant draft, English; Vietnamese translation still needed.**

You lead a voluntary training association in a city. The association has funds for twenty places in a course whose participants have comparable expected benefit. One hundred and fifty applicants meet the same entry standard. Fifty belong to a named local community that the association excluded from its courses for decades; fifty qualify under a current low-income threshold. Twenty applicants belong to both groups. Applicants have other differences, but no further ranking has been authorized.

The exclusion ended ten years ago. The association acknowledges the policy, and the relevant records are undisputed. It has not provided a remedy for that policy. Current intake procedures are accessible to everyone, and all applicants face the same entry standard. The rule for allocating places in this cohort is still to be selected from the authorized options below. Present low income is not treated as a complete record of past disadvantage.

Your members have authorized you to select one of three lawful rules. You cannot add places, lower the entry standard, identify applicants publicly, or cancel earlier admissions. Every lottery is independently run. The course has no safety-critical or professional licensing function.

| Option | Select | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Equal chance for every eligible applicant | Allocate all twenty places by a lottery among all 150 eligible applicants. | Neither the association's exclusion nor current low income receives additional priority. |
| B — Reserve places as a response to the exclusion | Allocate ten places by lottery among the fifty applicants from the formerly excluded community; allocate ten more among all applicants not already selected. | Some equally qualified applicants outside that community have fewer chances, including some low-income applicants. |
| C — Reserve places for current low income | Allocate ten places by lottery among the fifty low-income applicants; allocate ten more among all applicants not already selected. | Some members of the formerly excluded community receive no added priority, and the policy does not directly respond to the association's own history. |

**Decision:** Which rule would you use under your member mandate? Preserve uncertain, unrepresented, and skip responses.

**Unscored reasons to investigate:** equal procedure now; an institution's responsibility for its own wrongs; current material disadvantage; acknowledging a group without assuming all members are alike; preventing renewed hierarchy; concern about institutional power to classify people; a view that course admission is or is not the appropriate remedy. A might accompany strong support for a different form of compensation. B might express reparative responsibility or a practical effort to rebuild institutional trust. C might express sufficientarian need, class analysis, or concern about who benefits within each community. Do not equate a selection with a complete view of caste, race, colonialism, merit, or equality.

**One-factor variant:** replace only “It has not provided a remedy for that policy” with “Five years ago it completed a compensation agreement for the policy, negotiated with elected representatives of affected former applicants; the agreement did not decide future course admissions.” Keep the historical exclusion, current applicants, group counts, low-income distribution, course budget, authority, and allocation options fixed. This tests sensitivity to a prior institutional remedy. Agreement that the remedy actually settles an obligation is an interpretation to elicit, not a factual answer to impose. If “completed compensation” is heard as proof that all social disadvantage vanished, revise the wording.

**Cognitive probes:** Who was excluded—these applicants, earlier applicants, or both? Does the wording say that every member is currently poor? Can a person benefit under either reservation rule? What would count as repair, and is this course the appropriate vehicle? Avoid importing a real group label for a population-level inference. A dedicated study of a particular caste, racial, Indigenous, or postcolonial context needs its own informed review and cannot be substituted by this generic case.

## Content acceptance checks before coding

- A reviewer can identify the exact decision maker, approval route, money or time limit, relevant rights, and consequence of every package without importing a real country's law.
- Independent readers can explain a defensible reason for each option and at least two different motives for one option; no option wins merely by hiding its cost or promising unbounded effectiveness.
- Quantities balance: S01 moving grants are `10 × 9,000 = 90,000`; S02 each plan allocates exactly 40 extra weekly hours while recipients retain the right to refuse; S04 each rule awards exactly 100 grants without duplicate winners; S06 each rule awards exactly twenty places without duplicate winners. These arithmetic facts establish consistency, not equal political attractiveness.
- People understand the distinction between certainty and preference, baseline legal rights and additional powers, present needs and historical responsibility, and project-caused risk and background risk. Revise or remove a case if these distinctions consume the interview rather than support it.
- Both language versions preserve permissions, obligation words, quantities, exceptions, time horizons and uncertainty. Independent bilingual reviewers resolve terms such as `respite`, `voice`, `review`, `standing`, `redress` and `public fund`; mechanical translation completeness is insufficient.
- Reviewers list every changed and unchanged field for a proposed variant. If the variant changes a second substantive fact, disclose a package contrast or redesign it. Check respondent interpretations as well as literal text equality.
- Setting-specific review is completed for each cell actually selected. No cell becomes ready because another setting's note is complete; no real-country correspondence appears in participant-facing scenario or variant text before debrief.
- Retain unrepresented, uncertain and skipped answers. Keep reasons and personal preferences separate from role actions. Do not add an ideology classifier or fold these choices into the current four means without a separate, evidence-backed measurement design.

No participant interviews, expert approvals, linguistic equivalence findings, calibrated option scores, or country-law checks are claimed in this document. These are concrete drafts for the next content review, whose findings may justify revising, splitting or rejecting them.
