# Content quality and bilingual review protocol

Prepared 23 October 2026; revised 25 October 2026 for the content-research phase. **Author proposal; no new interviews, independent human reviews, translation adjudication, or empirical results are represented here.** The application remains instrument `0.4.0` with its existing 49-item route. This document makes content decisions reviewable before implementation; it changes no participant wording or scoring.

Use this alongside the [current-content audit](CURRENT-CONTENT-AUDIT.md), [36 candidate statements](CANDIDATE-BANK.md), [six scenario families](SCENARIO-LAB.md), and existing [pilot guide](../PILOT-GUIDE.md). The pilot guide retains responsibility for recruitment, consent, operator, recording, and data handling. The decisions below concern the quality and allocation of the material that those procedures would review.

## What higher density should mean

Aim for more interpretable distinctions per reading and decision task, not more statements per page. Before seeing participant data, this is an editorial aim, not an estimate of statistical information, reliability, or an item-response-theory parameter.

For each proposed item, reviewers should be able to complete these sentences:

1. “This response can tell us something about ___.”
2. “It adds ___ that the other items do not already ask.”
3. “Someone could reasonably answer the same way because ___ instead.”
4. “The following scenario fact or follow-up would help distinguish those readings: ___.”
5. “It cannot establish ___.”

An extra item earns its reading burden by adding a meaningful distinction, testing a competing explanation, or deliberately checking whether an interpretation travels to a different context. Repetition has a purpose only when that purpose is recorded. Do not remove conceptually necessary coverage merely because two items eventually correlate, or retain duplicates merely because they raise internal consistency.

The present audit identifies three immediate opportunities: replace repeated cultural-program questions with distinct claims; separate economic ownership, funding, access, and decision authority; and replace low-threshold “can” or “may” premises with propositions whose scope readers can actually contest. Stronger wording is not automatically better: a universal “always” can create an easy counterexample instead of a useful philosophical disagreement.

## Review the intended claim before judging its wording

| Claim layer | What the author must specify | What an answer does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social ontology or causal explanation | What entities or relations matter, the context being explained, and whether the statement concerns possibility, frequency, or relative explanatory importance. | That the respondent morally approves of the relation, or endorses a whole school of thought. |
| Normative commitment | The right, good, obligation, or priority at issue; whose claims count; any stated limit. | That the respondent believes a particular institution will realize it. |
| Political knowledge | The kind of evidence, knowledge source, or method of criticism being compared. | That a source is always reliable, or should automatically govern. |
| Institutional judgment | The authority or arrangement proposed, its scope, and relevant assumptions about how it works. | Whether disagreement concerns the principle, feasibility, expected effects, or trust in actual office holders. |
| Role action | The authorized decision, fixed facts, practical alternatives, and accountability. | A causal role effect, a respondent's authentic identity, or a contradiction with their personal values. |

Mixed claims need an explicit rationale. If an item asks whether worker control is both fair and efficient, split fairness from the empirical expectation or select one intended claim. The same participant can accept the normative principle while doubting the proposed mechanism.

## A practical rubric without a total quality score

For each criterion, record **no issue found**, **specific revision needed**, or **unresolved interpretation**, followed by the observed wording, competing reading, and proposed action. A polished translation cannot compensate for an infeasible decision; a strong citation cannot compensate for a compound question. Do not average these judgments into an approval score.

| Criterion | Required check | Concrete revision trigger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Added distinction | Compare with adjacent and existing items; state the additional conceptual information. | A new statement repeats “preserve old customs” with a different cultural example but adds no declared contrast. |
| One answerable claim | Ask whether agreement with one clause and disagreement with another are plausible. | Agreement requires simultaneously endorsing a moral end, a causal prediction, and an institution. |
| Scope and quantifiers | State what “usually,” “essential,” “independent,” or “major” means for this task. | Readers must invent incompatible scopes before they can answer. |
| Genuine contestability | Produce a charitable argument for agreement and disagreement without making either side ignorant or malicious. | The only imagined disagreement rejects an obvious fact or an attractive slogan. |
| Viewpoint fidelity | Ask an informed interpreter and an informed critic to reconstruct the strongest relevant account. | A tradition is reduced to its opponent's caricature, or one answer becomes a membership test. |
| Option comparability | Compare specificity, reading burden, benefits, costs, certainty, and legal availability. | One choice has a benefit and no cost; another is given only a moralizing label. |
| Authority and feasibility | Identify who acts, which approval is needed, and whether every option meets the shared constraints. | A constituent directly spends the public budget, or a declared affordable option cannot pay its stated costs. |
| Inferential restraint | List at least two motives that could produce the same response. | A cooperative choice is declared politically moderate, or a preference for review is treated as distrust of all expertise. |
| Translation equivalence | Compare the claim, actor, scope, burden, negation, response intensity, and permitted action in both languages. | Vietnamese makes an optional action mandatory or converts public responsibility into exclusive state operation. |
| Context and concealment | Ensure decision-relevant institutions are available but real-country correspondences and tradition labels are absent from participant material. | An institution is unexplained, a pre-debrief label reveals the inspiration, or concealment is described as secure blinding. |
| Response fit | Check whether a person can express neutrality, uncertainty, conditional assent, rejection of the options, or refusal without being assigned a false midpoint. | “It depends on a missing fact” is stored as political moderation. |

Use the existing critical/major/minor severity vocabulary. Invalid authority, infeasible choices, and a changed but unrecorded experimental fact are critical for affected material. Unsupported interpretation and recurring ambiguity normally need revision or explicit exclusion of the inference. Record the reason for a severity judgment; a political disagreement with the author is not itself a defect.

## Independent review with opposing readings

Prepare reviewer copies without revealing the item's intended ideological association first. Ask each reviewer to paraphrase the claim and identify competing interpretations. Then disclose the author rationale and source proposal, so the review records both an unprompted reading and a response to the intended construct.

For each conceptual dispute, seek complementary expertise: an informed interpreter of one relevant position, an informed critic or interpreter of a rival account, and bilingual wording review. These are functions, not fixed ideological quotas or evidence that one individual represents a whole tradition. A single person may perform more than one function, but their overlapping roles and lack of independence must be recorded. The author and AI-generated critiques cannot supply the independent review evidence.

Ask both conceptual reviewers to produce the strongest case for each side before suggesting wording. Preserve disagreement about the construct even if they agree on readable wording. If one reviewer says a proposition can be affirmed by apparently opposed traditions for different reasons, treat that as an important design finding rather than a failed attempt to produce a clean party label.

Keep political-tradition names in researcher material. For example, a disagreement about inherited obligations may involve conservative, communitarian, anti-colonial, religious, and liberal arguments without being exclusive to any one of them. A source map should document that overlap and relevant internal differences.

## Working English–Vietnamese terminology

These are **translation proposals to test**, not a completed glossary or approved Vietnamese participant text. Prefer an accessible explanation of the particular claim over an untranslated technical label. Retain contested alternatives in the reconciliation record.

| English distinction | Vietnamese wording to consider | Risk and probe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social ontology | Researcher label: **bản thể luận xã hội**; participant explanation: **cách hiểu về con người và các quan hệ xã hội**. | The explanation is broader and easier than the technical term. Ask whether the sentence describes society or says how it ought to be. |
| Agency | **khả năng tự quyết định và hành động**, tailored to the specific action. | **Năng lực hành động** may sound like practical skill or legal capacity. Ask whether the claim concerns independence, ability, or effective opportunity. |
| Liberty/freedom | **tự do**, with the exact condition spelled out. | “No prohibition,” “real opportunity,” and “not dependent on arbitrary permission” are different claims; the common noun does not make them equivalent. |
| Non-domination | **không phải lệ thuộc vào quyền can thiệp tùy ý của người khác**. | **Không bị thống trị** can suggest only a state or colonial ruler; the item may also concern an employer, household, or landlord. Probe a case where power exists but is not exercised. |
| State | **nhà nước**. | Do not substitute **xã hội** or assume it means the current government alone. |
| Government/public authority | **chính phủ** for the national governing body; **chính quyền** or a named agency for the relevant authority. | Specify level and body. A local official is not the national cabinet. |
| Public provision/public responsibility | Depending on the claim, **cung ứng dịch vụ công** or **trách nhiệm bảo đảm dịch vụ cho mọi người**; state operation: **do cơ quan nhà nước trực tiếp vận hành**. | Public funding, regulation, ownership, and operation must remain distinct. Ask who pays, owns, and decides. |
| Collective/community ownership | **sở hữu tập thể** or **sở hữu của cộng đồng**, with the actual owners named. | Neither automatically means state ownership or equal decision power. Name members, residents, workers, or the legal entity. |
| Authority/power | **thẩm quyền** for authorized decision rights; **quyền lực** for broader capacity to influence or constrain. | “Has the authority” can become “has influence” if translated loosely. Ask who can lawfully make the final decision in this fiction. |
| Coercion | **cưỡng chế** for an institutional enforcement power; **ép buộc** where the claim concerns pressure on a person. | Legal enforcement and economic dependence are not interchangeable; decide which is intended. |
| Accountability/contestability | **trách nhiệm giải trình**; explain a practical ability to **yêu cầu xem xét lại hoặc phản đối quyết định**. | Being allowed to complain does not necessarily mean effective independent review or a power to reverse the decision. |
| Care/dependency | **chăm sóc** and **sự phụ thuộc vào việc chăm sóc**, naming the task or dependency. | **Quan tâm** can mean concern or attention rather than care work; do not assume dependency is always familial or permanent. |
| Class/caste | Economic class: **giai cấp** or a concrete economic position; caste: **đẳng cấp cha truyền con nối**, with an appropriate contextual explanation. | Caste is not simply income inequality. Ask whether readers understand inherited status and social closure; obtain specialist review before settling wording. |
| Desert/merit | Specify **đóng góp**, **thành tích**, **nỗ lực**, or another intended basis. | **Xứng đáng** leaves the basis of deservingness unstated; effort, talent, output, and need are different claims. |
| Community/nation/citizenship/residency | Name **cộng đồng**, **quốc gia**, **tư cách công dân**, or **việc cư trú** as applicable. | **Dân tộc** can suggest ethnicity as well as nation. Do not silently turn residents into citizens or cultural membership into ancestry. |
| Tradition/historical repair | **truyền thống** or **tập quán** for the particular practice; a concrete description of **khắc phục hậu quả của sự bất công trong quá khứ**. | Preserving a practice is different from endorsing inherited hierarchy; historical repair is different from assigning individual guilt. |

Ask reviewers to flag unexplained loanwords, institutional language that sounds like a real national-law claim, and expressions that become more deferential or accusatory in one language. A neat word-for-word match can still change the task.

## Response categories to settle before coding

For candidate statement review, begin with five ordered agreement labels plus explicit uncertainty and skipping. Draft paired labels are:

| English | Vietnamese author draft |
| --- | --- |
| Strongly agree | Hoàn toàn đồng ý |
| Somewhat agree | Phần nào đồng ý |
| Neither agree nor disagree | Không nghiêng về đồng ý hay phản đối |
| Somewhat disagree | Phần nào không đồng ý |
| Strongly disagree | Hoàn toàn không đồng ý |
| Not sure how to answer | Chưa chắc nên trả lời thế nào |
| Prefer to skip | Muốn bỏ qua câu này |

The Vietnamese intensity labels need review: **hoàn toàn** can indicate absolute agreement, while “strongly” may indicate strength without absolute scope. Test an alternative such as **rất đồng ý** if readers interpret the endpoints differently. Do not decide equivalence from apparent symmetry alone.

During interviews, distinguish four readings that the middle of a scale can conceal: genuine indifference between positions; simultaneous agreement with different parts; conditional assent; and lack of understanding or relevant knowledge. A conditional response should have its condition recorded separately in the interview, not be silently scored as neutral or uncertain. If it recurs because an item omits a decision-relevant fact, revise the item. If conditionality is itself the intended construct, draft an explicit conditional question for later review instead of adding an ambiguous general “depends” score.

For scenarios, retain choices, uncertainty, “none of these represents my decision,” and skipping as different records. Ask for a principal reason without making each reason exclusive to one action. In interviews, allow the person to describe a missing reason; do not compel disclosure of a personal political justification. A secondary reason can reveal mixed motives, but do not add it to every future task without evidence that its value justifies the burden.

A personal-preference follow-up must retain the same facts, resources, permissions, and feasible packages. Record it separately from the role action. Neither response supplies a ground truth about the person's real ideology. Before a quantitative study, examine whether asking action then preference creates consistency pressure; the current fixed order cannot resolve this.

## Exact draft allocation for content interviews

Use three complementary packets to inspect the new bank. Each contains **12 candidate statements: four complete triads of three statements**, followed by **two scenario families**, for **14 primary response tasks**. Probes, briefing, optional reasons, and the debrief add burden and are not included in that count. The allocation is a content-coverage proposal, not an implemented form system or a claim of equal difficulty.

| Packet | Candidate triads: include A, B, and C in each | Scenario families | Main review emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | `CR-02` power; `CR-03` freedom; `CR-05` enterprise authority; `CR-06` knowledge | `CR-S01` housing governance; `CR-S03` contested public evidence | Ownership versus control, alternative meanings of freedom, and reasons for trusting evidence. |
| B | `CR-01` agency/social formation; `CR-04` desert/need/care; `CR-11` ecological standing; `CR-12` hierarchy/coordination | `CR-S02` respite-care allocation; `CR-S05` ecological risk and burdens | Dependency, moral standing, and the relation between coordination and authority. |
| C | `CR-07` continuity/reform; `CR-08` membership; `CR-09` religious/public justification; `CR-10` status and historical repair | `CR-S04` community-benefit fund; `CR-S06` historical exclusion and training places | Membership boundaries, inherited claims, equal treatment, and corrective institutions. |

This covers all 36 candidate statements and six scenario families once across the packet set. It does not give each person a comprehensive ideology profile. Keep the triad intact within its packet for analysis, but test a presentation that separates closely related items instead of announcing a philosophical dispute and inviting deliberate consistency. Record actual presentation order.

In packet A, **CR-S03 is held as a comprehension probe pending redesign**, because its prescribed research purpose can make the response a matter of method suitability. Its inclusion in this qualitative packet does not make it an ideological indicator or approve it for a future assessment form.

Each participant sees one version of each allocated scenario. To examine a controlled variant in content interviews, prepare a counterpart packet that replaces one scenario's base version with its stated variant while preserving all other wording. Record packet, scenario version, language order, fictional world, and the actual facts shown. This is qualitative coverage, not causal identification or formal random allocation. A direct side-by-side invariance check belongs in reviewer work; it is not an independent unprimed participant response.

The first two scenario families have complete bilingual worked drafts; the other four require equivalent drafting and bilingual review before using Vietnamese versions. Do not fill this gap by treating an interpreter's improvisation as a fixed translation. Choose language order for each interview deliberately and record switches. Reading both languages does not provide two independent observations.

Before each round, maintain a coverage ledger of packet × scenario version × language × fictional setting actually reviewed. Use coverage gaps and observed problems to choose the next round; an arbitrary participant count does not establish complete coverage. The existing pilot guide's 12–20 readers is a planning estimate, not a guarantee that this expanded design has sufficient coverage. Fictional settings can be allocated in stages rather than forcing every possible combination into one small round.

For a later assessment, a **provisional budget of 18 statements plus six scenario decisions = 24 primary tasks** is a reasonable design constraint to investigate. A possible structure is six common statement anchors plus 12 rotating statements, with six scenarios selected from the combined existing and new bank after a coverage review. Use only one reviewed version of each selected family per person; do not automatically select all six new families and lose existing dissent or international-obligation coverage. Select anchors only after conceptual and comprehension review; do not designate them simply because they are already implemented. The number is a burden budget, not a recommended validated instrument length or a promise of 24 equally informative tasks. Common items alone do not establish form equivalence, individual comparability, or measurement invariance. No future form or score is approved by this document.

## Neutral cognitive probes

Ask for an unassisted answer first. Then use selected probes appropriate to the issue; a full interrogation after every item would defeat the burden goal.

| Distinction | Probe | What to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Description versus prescription | “Is this sentence describing how society works, saying how it should work, or doing something else?” | The participant's reading before explaining the author intention. |
| Social formation versus inability to dissent | “Can someone in the situation you described change their mind? What would make that possible?” | Whether a relational account is being read as complete determinism. |
| Principle versus expected effect | “Would your answer differ if this arrangement produced the same practical results as the alternative?” | A hypothetical probe, not a changed primary answer or proof of the motive. |
| Non-interference versus non-domination | “Does it matter that the other person has this power if they have never used it?” | Actual interference, standing dependence, fear of future use, or another reading. |
| Equality, need, effort, and entitlement | “Which difference between the people matters to your answer? Which would not matter?” | The named criterion, without rewarding any particular principle. |
| Evidence versus decision authority | “Does trusting this source mean that the source should make the final decision?” | Separation or conflation of knowledge and governing power. |
| Role constraint versus personal preference | “Which facts still apply when you put the assigned responsibility aside?” | Invented money, altered legal permissions, or genuinely different priorities under the same constraints. |
| Missing facts versus disagreement | “Was there information you needed before you could choose?” | The missing fact and whether different plausible values would change the answer. |
| Response category meaning | “What did this response option mean to you here?” | Neutrality, conditionality, mixed claims, uncertainty, or refusal. |
| Bilingual meaning | “Do these two versions ask the same thing? Which word or phrase changes the decision?” | Specific scope, authority, intensity, or conceptual differences; not a generic fluency rating. |
| Debrief limits | “What can this result tell you, and what cannot it tell you?” | Ideology-label, national-population, causal-effect, or contradiction claims the reader inferred. |

Do not prompt country guesses during the decision task. After the final reveal, ask whether the person had already inferred an inspiration and whether it affected their reading. Keep factual institutional constraints visible before the reveal. Delayed labels reduce one source of priming; they do not prevent inference or source inspection.

## Proposed scoring decisions before the next implementation

These recommendations concern a future reviewed version; the current scoring remains unchanged.

- Keep all new premise candidates descriptive at item level. Do not add ideology distances or treat the 12 disputes as established bipolar dimensions.
- Reclassify or withhold civil-domain aggregation for `civil-records` and `civil-withholding` until the content can distinguish transparency, administrative burden, and civil-protection commitments. A willingness to do more paperwork is not an established freedom–order measure.
- Treat the housing cooperative, mixed service provision, and split cultural budgets as distinct institutional choices. A numerical midpoint does not establish moderation; revise the rubric before describing them that way.
- Examine economic funding, ownership, access, and decision authority separately; examine national obligation, practical cooperation, and decision sovereignty separately. The present domains may remain editorial navigation categories without becoming validated scales.
- Retain scenario choices and reasons descriptively while their intended constructs and alternative explanations are unresolved. A scene with a genuine trade-off can be useful content even when it should not contribute to an axis score.
- Keep comparisons and optional preferences out of substantive totals. Do not substitute agreement between statements and decisions for evidence of validity, or disagreement for hypocrisy.

If later evidence supports a summary, document its target construct, item selection, missing-response handling, intended population/language, alternative models, and limitations before implementing it. Internal consistency alone cannot validate a heterogeneous political inventory; a formative inventory, a reflective scale, and a descriptive typology need different arguments and evidence.

## Content gates for the next build

The next implementation should use a frozen content decision record, not the entire drafting folder. For every selected statement or scenario, require:

1. A stable candidate ID, claim type, rationale, rival reading, inference limit, and source status. Proposed citations remain proposed until the text and relevant context have actually been checked.
2. A clear disposition: adopt, revise, retain for further review, or reject, with the reason. A rejected item remains in the history so its ambiguity is not accidentally reintroduced later.
3. An internally coherent role, permissions, facts, benefits, costs, and options; a documented invariant list for any variant; no unresolved critical content fault in the chosen material.
4. Separate English and Vietnamese version records, independent review functions, reconciliation decisions, and remaining comprehension questions. A machine-generated bilingual draft does not close this gate.
5. A declared participant budget and form coverage, a response-category decision, and an explicit descriptive or justified scoring treatment. Preserve country correspondences for the end debrief.

This protocol does not claim those gates have been completed. It identifies what the current research preparation makes concrete and what requires source access or human evidence. New source, wording, or translation findings should update the decision record before coding resumes.
