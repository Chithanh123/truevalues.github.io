# Political Context: research specification draft

Instrument: `political-context-pilot`, version `0.4.0`.

This prototype is an instrument under development. Its items, world briefs, and scoring rubric have not undergone an external expert review, cognitive interviews, a completed literature review, or psychometric validation. Technical coverage checks establish that the authored content can run; they do not establish the validity of its measures. No research participants have been recruited and no study data are collected by the application.

## Current content and intended interpretation

The instrument contains 24 candidate value statements, six in each of four provisional domains; 16 additional descriptive foundation statements; eight main scenarios, two authored for each content domain; and one repeated housing scenario in which the school-repair deadline changes. The full route has 49 primary response items, plus optional reasons and personal preferences. A provisional 25–35 minute completion estimate requires observation in the comprehension pilot. Seven main scenarios contribute provisional directional scores. The eighth, `public-records`, is descriptive-only and contributes no score or denominator to any domain. Every route includes the five requested roles: ordinary constituent, civil society leader, non-elected public servant/local official, local leader, and national leader. It includes urban and rural surroundings.

This is an author-review document and contains country inspirations. Participants who wish to preserve the delayed reveal should finish their assessment before inspecting this document or the separate reviewer workspace.

A route uses one randomly assigned fictional setting. The eight settings draw selected institutional features from the United States, United Kingdom, Germany, Japan, Fiji, Viet Nam, India, and the Central African Republic. The same anchor dilemmas appear across settings, with an authored note explaining the institutional context. These are fictional profile packages, not representative descriptions of real countries or their populations. The stated scenario powers and constraints govern the decision; recognizing the inspiration should not be necessary. Explicit correspondence labels are withheld from participant pages and dialogs until the final debrief. Institutional facts remain available throughout. The separate author-review workspace and public static source still disclose mappings, so this is a presentation rule rather than secure experimental blinding. Names or institutions may also permit inference; delayed labels do not establish that participants were unaware of a correspondence.

The current route and option order are fixed. It is not a balanced experimental design, a full factorial study, or a representative sample of the possible dilemmas in each society. The eight-world by five-role by two-locality space contains 80 context cells before adding dilemmas, but this version does not claim to cover every cell with an independently authored story.

The first comprehension-pilot audience is **bilingual English–Vietnamese adults**, as chosen for this development phase. Recruitment geography, exact age and language-proficiency eligibility, responsible operator, primary outcomes, and data handling remain undecided. This audience choice does not establish representativeness of either language population.

Every item and setting remains an **author draft**. The [pilot guide](PILOT-GUIDE.md) and [empty review log](review-log-template.csv) support the next review stage. They contain no findings or invented participant records. AI-assisted technical and content checks are development work; the empty log does not represent completed independent human review.

The [design matrix](DESIGN-MATRIX.md) records every statement and situation, its intended rationale, rival explanations, scoring status, and authored world coverage. The [progress ledger](PROGRESS.md) separates software integration and prepared materials from completed research and public-site verification.

## Research question and candidate outcomes

The proposed central question is: **How do explicitly stated political values and underlying assumptions relate to decisions made under specified responsibilities and constraints?**

| Proposed question | Candidate outcome | Limits to specify in a protocol |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How do people interpret premises about society, knowledge, values, and institutions? | Item-level foundation responses and paraphrases | The new items are descriptive probes, not validated ontology scales or political-identity indicators. |
| Do value endorsements relate to decisions in the same content domain? | Association between a defined statement summary and a chosen action | Categories and rubrics require validation; association does not identify a cause. |
| How do people respond to a changed repair deadline? | Housing action in each version and whether the recorded actions differ | The current repeated, fixed-order presentation permits recognition, practice, and consistency effects. |
| Do participants understand the scope of their assigned powers? | Correct paraphrase of who decides, what is permitted, and what is constrained | This is a comprehension outcome; it does not measure political sophistication or ideology. |
| Which item wording or contextual assumptions generate uncertainty? | Item-level uncertainty, skips, unrepresented choices, and interview observations | Missing responses have different meanings; they should not be collapsed into ideological neutrality. |
| How do personal preferences relate to actions selected within an assigned responsibility? | Separately recorded preference and role action for the same feasible packages | Optional answers and fixed action-first order can affect the contrast; it is not a causal role effect or a measure of hypocrisy. |
| Can people understand the debrief without overinterpreting it? | Explanation of what a marker, coverage count, and comparison do and do not imply | A completed test or an attractive result screen does not demonstrate measurement validity. |

These are protocol candidates, not preregistered hypotheses. Before a study, choose a small set of primary outcomes and contrasts, define an analysis and exclusion plan, and justify the sample size for those choices. Preserve a separate exploratory analysis category.

## Descriptive reflection report

Application release `0.4.2` organizes all 49 existing primary responses into seven author-chosen themes. Each item appears once in the theme report; the optional full records and housing comparison provide additional ways to inspect the same answers. The themes are navigation and discussion aids, not seven scales, latent constructs, ideology affinities, or causal explanations. No candidate items from the content-research pack are activated by this release.

| Report theme | Existing response coverage | Main interpretation limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ownership, provision and control | Six value statements, three premises, three situations including the housing comparison | Funding, ownership, access, control, and delivery constraints remain distinct. |
| Freedoms and contesting power | Four value statements, three premises, one gathering situation | One gathering decision cannot establish preferences across all institutions or restrictions. |
| Transparency and access to records | Two value statements and the descriptive records situation | Additional access, usability, and staff capacity are separate from public-order restrictions. The original civil profile still includes these two statements. |
| Belonging, obligations and cooperation | Six value statements, three premises, two situations | Answers do not establish which view of agency or belonging explains a decision. |
| Continuity and inclusion | Six value statements, one premise, two cultural situations | Cultural-program choices do not establish a general attitude to social change. |
| Knowledge and judgment | Four premises; no dedicated situation | Premise answers do not show how conflicting sources of knowledge would be weighed. |
| Care and ecological dependence | Two premises; no dedicated situation | One item about each topic cannot identify a care system, environmental policy, or position on nature's moral standing. |

The report displays exact recorded agreement labels, role actions, selected reasons, and optional preferences. It does not infer an unstated reason, treat an absent preference as agreement, or diagnose consistency between linked answers. Neutral agreement responses count as definite responses for coverage; uncertainty, skips, and unrepresented choices remain distinct. Coverage is not confidence, accuracy, or reliability. The generic reflection prompts collect no additional answers.

The original four provisional profiles remain available in a collapsed optional view with their unchanged rubric and denominators. Moving transparency into a separate reading section does not resolve the original civil-profile confound or silently rescore its statements. The housing contrast remains descriptive. The final debrief retains the delayed country reveal; incomplete sessions cannot generate the new reflection report.

Participants can download a plain-text reflection report or use the browser's print / save-as-PDF dialog. These views include recorded answers and the revealed setting, without new scores. The original JSON response export remains available with its unchanged version `2` format and summaries. Presentation release `0.4.2` retains instrument `0.4.0`, all question wording, response semantics, and compatible saved sessions. Report comprehension still requires independent human review and interviews.

## Candidate construct map

| Domain | Left endpoint | Right endpoint | Important limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Economic | Market allocation | Public provision | Redistribution, ownership, and service delivery may be distinct constructs. |
| Diplomatic | Domestic priority | Cross-border cooperation | Local obligations, internationalism, and practical benefits are not equivalent. |
| Civil | Public-order powers | Civil protections | Administrative capacity is not coercion; complying with a duty is not endorsement. |
| Societal | Cultural continuity | Cultural change | Continuity, pluralism, equal opportunity, and attitudes to particular customs may differ. |

Review political theory and existing measurement literature before adopting a final scale structure. The matrix flags `civil-records` and `civil-withholding` as priority cases: those statement rubrics still require review because transparency and staff capacity need not indicate civil-rights or public-order preferences. Prepare a source-by-construct map, identify omissions and rival interpretations, and obtain independent rubric review across political viewpoints. More items alone do not establish reliability, dimensionality, or comparability between statement and scenario summaries.

## Broader ideological-premise aims

Version 0.4 adds a separate framework for examining assumptions that policy-level statements can leave implicit. The aim is a more comprehensive, source-traceable bank spanning disagreements within and across political traditions, rather than treating “leftist” or “liberal” as a single ontology. The [foundations framework](FOUNDATIONS.md) documents the candidate map and item rationales.

The draft distinguishes four kinds of question:

| Category | Question being explored | Interpretation boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social ontology | What kinds of persons, relationships, groups, structures, and dependencies make up social life? | Describing a social relation does not itself prescribe a policy. |
| Normative premises | Which goods, obligations, freedoms, and forms of equality deserve priority? | Shared values can support different actions under different empirical assumptions. |
| Epistemology | Whose knowledge is relevant, and how should claims and uncertainty be assessed? | Support for a knowledge source does not settle its reliability in every setting. |
| Institutions | How should power, coordination, accountability, and collective action be organized? | An institutional preference need not imply one exclusive philosophical foundation. |

The 16 candidate items are a small starting bank. Political-tradition associations are overlapping author-review metadata, with internal disagreements and cautions. No tradition receives a similarity score, and no response is converted into a leftist, liberal, or other identity. The four existing policy domains cannot stand in for this wider framework.

Next aims are to verify primary and measurement sources; document rival interpretations; obtain independent review from different traditions and relevant cultural settings; expand underrepresented premises; and develop balanced forms only after burden and comprehension are understood. A larger bank must preserve item-level provenance, wording history, category rationale, and evidence for any proposed summary. It must be tested for dimensionality and cross-language interpretation before proposing ontology profiles or comparing groups. The present draft is not exhaustive or a completed literature synthesis. The separate [source-proposal map](SOURCES.md) creates an item-level verification queue, with proposed background/contrast links and open questions. Retrieval of the initial source texts was blocked in the development environment; those proposals remain unverified. Verify authorship, exact sections, relevance, and rival readings against the actual publications before relying on them. Neither a proposed citation nor a later successful text check is evidence that a source endorses the item wording or that a tradition has one shared position.

## Provisional scoring contract

The original 24 value-statement agreement responses run from -2 to +2 and are multiplied by the statement's recorded `direction`. A reverse-keyed item aligns endorsement of the left endpoint with the same display direction. The instrument has six candidate value statements per domain. The 16 foundation items use the same response categories for accessibility, but have no direction, domain score, average, or ideology classifier. Their individual answers, uncertainty, and skips are retained separately in the debrief and response export and contribute no weight or denominator to the four profiles.

Each of the seven directionally scored main scenarios has one provisional domain and three authored option scores: -2, 0, and +2. A zero score describes the author's intermediate package in that situation. It does not establish that the participant is politically centrist. Each scored scenario has equal maximum influence within its domain. Statements and scenarios are summarized separately.

The `public-records` scenario has `scoring: "descriptive"` and null option scores. Transparency, information usability, and staff capacity have not been established as indicators on the civil-rights/public-order axis. Its actual choices and reasons remain in the evidence and export, but it is excluded from every domain aggregate and denominator. Economic, diplomatic, and societal profiles therefore each have two scored main scenarios; the civil profile has one. Coverage counts must disclose this asymmetry.

Uncertain, skipped, and unrepresented responses are excluded from means, while their original response types remain available. A genuine neutral statement response or midpoint action remains included. Reasons are explanatory labels and do not change scores. Optional personal-preference responses are also unscored and excluded from every profile denominator; their raw response categories remain separate from role actions and reasons. The repeated housing variant is excluded from the main scenario profile to avoid double-weighting that dilemma.

| Main scenario ID | Provisional scoring direction | Important rival explanations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `housing-urgent` | Sale toward market allocation; municipal construction toward public provision; cooperative lease at the authored midpoint | Immediate revenue, delivery time, school urgency, and ownership may motivate different choices. A cooperative is not inherently a midpoint in every economic theory. |
| `public-assembly` | Relocation toward public-order restrictions; existing freedoms toward civil protections; time/sound conditions at the midpoint | A preference for predictable enforcement is different from endorsement of coercion in general. All options use the same fictional lawful premise. |
| `water-partnership` | Local-only allocation toward domestic priority; larger partnership allocation toward cross-border cooperation; shared allocation at the midpoint | Practical learning, local mandate, distribution, and moral obligations can produce the same action. |
| `community-festival` | Protected established program toward continuity; open program toward change; split program at the midpoint | The dilemma concerns festival funding, rather than anyone's legal rights. Equal-opportunity preferences can overlap with cultural-change preferences. |
| `service-contract` | Contracted provision toward market allocation; direct public operation toward public provision; mixed provision at the midpoint | Reliability, contract oversight, skills, and implementation capacity may matter independently of ownership preferences. |
| `community-history` | Established emphasis toward continuity; revising the exhibition toward change; a combined approach at the midpoint | Representing members, stewardship of a collection, and public appeal are not interchangeable with attitudes toward social change generally. |
| `public-records` | Descriptive-only: all option scores are null; excluded from domain aggregates and denominators | All plans meet the same legal floor. The responses concern transparency, usability, and staff capacity. Retain choices and reasons for review without treating them as civil-rights or public-order scores. |
| `regional-cooperation` | Domestic-first allocation toward domestic priority; larger shared regional commitment toward cooperation; a split package at the midpoint | Expected benefits, national responsibilities, implementation risk, and cross-border obligations can influence the allocation. |

Exact option IDs and scores are published in `js/content.js`. Display actual selections, unscored reasons, and evidence counts alongside summaries. The domains have only one or two scored main situations, which still provide limited coverage. Do not publish population percentiles, confidence percentages, authoritative ideology labels, or a numeric hypocrisy or belief/action-gap score.

## Personal preference versus role action

Version 0.3 adds an optional personal-preference response after a situation's role action. It uses the same feasible packages, facts, resources, and constraints while asking the person to put the assigned responsibility aside. It does not invite unlimited spending, changed legal permissions, different safety facts, or an alternative invented world.

Store and display preference independently of the chosen role action and reason. A missing, uncertain, or unrepresented preference is not evidence that the person personally endorses their role action. Preferences do not change ideology summaries, coverage counts, or the comparison of actions in the housing pair. This remains true for the descriptive public-records exercise.

The debrief may describe what was recorded, such as choosing one package in the role and preferring another personally. It cannot establish that the role caused the difference, identify a more authentic ideology, or diagnose hypocrisy. Additional reflection, wording, responsibilities, or uncertainty could explain it. Because the preference prompt follows the action, it may also introduce consistency pressure and demand effects. Review these interpretations in the comprehension pilot before selecting any study outcome.

## Bilingual author review

`review.html` shows English source content alongside draft Vietnamese translations and supports separate local reviewer worksheets. Authored content is immutable within the workspace; notes do not change the instrument. It covers the value and foundation banks, conceptual categories and political-tradition review lenses, contextual situations and choices, fictional world briefs, and institutional notes. It is explicitly marked as an author workspace with country-correspondence spoilers. The participant assessment remains English. This workspace is not a complete Vietnamese participant version, a validated translation, a recruitment form, or a response-collection service.

Vietnamese text in `js/translations/vi-items.js`, `js/translations/vi-foundations.js`, and `js/translations/vi-worlds.js` is an author draft awaiting independent bilingual review. Do not infer semantic equivalence from complete field coverage, fluent wording, or a passing technical check. Keep the English source version, translation revision, disputed terms, and reconciled decisions identifiable.

Use independent forward review, a separate bilingual review perspective, reconciliation, and cognitive probing in both languages. Back-translation may reveal discrepancies but is not sufficient validation by itself. Review role authority, negation, quantifiers, obligation versus discretion, non-sensitive versus protected information, response intensity, and neutral versus uncertain categories. The protocol is detailed in [PILOT-GUIDE.md](PILOT-GUIDE.md).

Bilingual adult readers are the first intended review audience, not a representative cross-national sample. Their answers cannot establish equivalence for monolingual populations. Record language order and any switching during interviews; reading both versions or thinking aloud can itself influence interpretation and subsequent answers.

## Reviewer worksheets and source traceability

Reviewer tooling release `0.4.1` adds notes without changing instrument `0.4.0`, the 49-item route, scoring, or compatible participant saves. The reviewer-note schema has its own version. A note identifies a stable target, the selected fictional world for a scenario, language, field, criterion, severity, observation, proposed change, source note, outcome, and resolution record. Outcomes are `revise`, `discuss`, and `no-issue`; they record a reviewer's finding, not an institutional approval or completed validation.

Opening content does not create a review record. An explicit no-issue entry helps distinguish considered content from content never reviewed, but coverage counts cannot establish that every field, perspective, translation issue, or study cell has been examined. Preserve a round-level allocation and coverage record alongside notes.

Each entry stores a fingerprint of the authored English/Vietnamese target and applicable context. Valid notes against changed content are marked stale on restore/import and require explicit rechecking. The fingerprint is a noncryptographic change identifier rather than an identity, authenticity, or integrity guarantee. Instrument-version mismatches and malformed note files are rejected without silently replacing the current draft. A validated import previews replacement and requires the reviewer to confirm it.

Notes stay in memory by default; optional device persistence uses `political-context-review-notes`, separate from assessment progress. JSON exports support continued editing; CSV exports neutralize spreadsheet-formula prefixes while preserving the original note text in JSON. The CSV retains the original sixteen log columns and adds language, target kind and field, source fingerprint, source note, and update time. Fields not represented in the worksheet, such as review round and final resolved-in version, must be completed through the operator's reconciliation record rather than inferred by software.

The authored paired packet excludes local notes. Reviewer-note exports exclude participant answers. Neither is a consented interview dataset, and notes should omit unnecessary political opinions, participant identities, and contact details. Device deletion and downloaded files remain separate concerns.

## Controlled housing contrast

The urgent and planned-repair cases retain the world, role, powers, housing need, borrowing limit, construction quantities, delivery periods, option wording, scores, and reasons. Their institutional notes are identical within a world. Only the engineering deadline and consequences of delaying school repairs change.

The urgent version says repairs must begin this year to avoid classroom closures. The comparison says repairs can safely wait until the next budget cycle. The shared option data and common facts make changes inspectable; the content validator also checks preserved fields.

The repeated case always appears later in this version. Recognition, order, practice, consistency pressure, or a changed interpretation can affect the answer. Report the observed pair descriptively. A formal urgency-effect study needs a justified design, such as counterbalanced or between-participant variants, and an analysis that respects the assignment.

## Assignment, coverage, and confounding

A seed reproducibly assigns one world and is recorded in the local session and voluntary export. This is adequate for inspecting local routes, but it is not a formal allocation protocol: people can restart, choose whether to complete, and keep or discard exports. There is no managed recruitment, allocation balance, duplicate-submission system, or response collection.

Shared anchor situations allow reviewers to compare the same authored dilemma across settings, but do not guarantee equivalent meaning or difficulty. For Namoa and Hoshima, the river-cooperation notes explicitly stipulate an invented shared-island basin for the fictional exercise; this is not a claim about the real inspirations' geography. Whole-world profiles bundle institutions and expectations. An observed profile effect would concern those fictional packages, not real nationality or the effect of one isolated institution. Assigned setting must not be used to infer a participant's nationality.

Role titles also bundle powers, obligations, and accountability. A public servant's authorized discretion differs from a national leader's proposal power. Unless those components are controlled, describe a contrast as an assigned-role package. The current route associates each dilemma with one role; it cannot isolate a pure role effect independently of dilemma content.

The current implemented route remains fixed, with no balanced form schedule. Candidate counterbalanced and between-participant forms are proposals in the design matrix, not deployed study allocation.

Value statements, then foundation statements, appear before contextual decisions as part of the intended experience. The added premise section can prime conceptual interpretations even without showing political-tradition labels. Later choices can reflect explicit value reflection or consistency pressure. Record this interpretation and evaluate order effects separately if the research question requires unprimed decisions.

## Review and pilot sequence

1. Operationalize the chosen bilingual English–Vietnamese adult audience: define geography, age/eligibility, language proficiency, operator, intended claims, primary questions, and data handling before recruitment.
2. Complete a literature-informed construct map and independent content/rubric review. Review every setting with people familiar with its inspiration. Independently review and reconcile the Vietnamese author draft with bilingual reviewers before using it as participant material.
3. Use the cognitive-interview guide to test comprehension, powers, feasibility, hidden assumptions, and debrief interpretation. Record issues rather than inventing validation findings.
4. Revise and version the content. Pilot burden, missingness, assignment fidelity, and candidate item behavior under an approved protocol.
5. Freeze the instrument and preregister a confirmatory design and analysis. Justify sample size using the planned contrasts and repeated-measures structure.
6. Use independent or held-out data for confirmation. Assess reliability where stability is intended, validity evidence, and relevant measurement comparability before group or language comparisons.
7. Publish the protocol, rubrics, analysis code, aggregate results, limitations, and unsupported as well as supported claims.

See [PILOT-GUIDE.md](PILOT-GUIDE.md) for the draft interview sequence and decision gates. Human reviews, interviews, recruitment, and empirical validation remain outstanding.

## Privacy and data handling

The application is local-only: no advertising, analytics, research submission endpoint, or automatic transmission of political responses. Default progress uses the current tab's session storage; optional device saving uses local storage. Erasure removes this tab's data and the saved device copy. Other open tabs may retain separate copies.

The v0.4 response export is a voluntary download containing sensitive political answers, including separate foundation responses, optional personal preferences, reasons, assigned world, instrument version, and summaries. The `0.4.2` readable text and printed/PDF reflection reports also contain recorded political responses and the setting revealed after completion. They are generated locally without a submission endpoint. Participants control whether to download, print, or share them. Erasing browser storage cannot erase files already downloaded or shared. Do not place responses or identifiable study IDs in shareable URLs.

The review-packet download is different: it contains authored instrument and review metadata, without a participant's responses or local reviewer notes. Reviewer worksheets have their own optional device save, download, and clear controls; the assessment erase control does not clear that separate draft. It supports content review and should not be represented as a research response submission.

Formal collection requires a separate consented workflow with a named operator, justified fields, access controls, retention, transport security, and an accurate withdrawal/deletion policy. Review hosting logs as well as application behavior. Keep incentive contact details separate, collect no research telemetry before consent, and obtain institutional ethics review where applicable. Public participant-level data require separate consideration of consent and disclosure risk.
